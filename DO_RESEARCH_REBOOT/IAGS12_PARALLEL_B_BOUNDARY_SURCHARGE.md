## 5. Short-scan and physical local-tree theorem analysis

### 5.1 A physical domain that preserves actual rotations

Fix one irregular fragment and let K be its ell distinct consecutive S keys. Let T be its actual initial B tree and define

\[
U=\bigcup_{x\in K}\operatorname{Anc}_T(x),
\tag{5.1}
\]

including each key itself. All these physical ancestors belong to S. U contains the actual root and is ancestor-closed. Its induced operand tree has the **same actual physical parent-child edges** between its vertices; no ancestor shortcut is inserted. U is generally not the key set of a complete rooted physical subtree: it is an ancestor-closed operand domain with complete U-free physical components attached at its fringe.

Keep every such component in the original tree. An access to K cannot enter a U-free component. If a rotation's middle component contains a U vertex, its physical root is in U by ancestor closure; if it contains none, it remains a complete attached hanger. Literal left rotation

    g(L,p(H,R)) -> p(g(L,H),R)

and its reflection move each complete middle component and preserve all its internal edges. The changed physical edges are the actual external, pivot and middle rows of RG01; an empty comparison slot does not erase a nonempty physical hanger or its changed incoming edge.

Induction on each **real primitive bit** proves that U stays ancestor-closed, target paths in the full tree and operand tree have exactly the same vertices, and each original double/zig uses exactly the same pivots in the same grouping. This retains all RR/LL/LR/RL cases and all physical middle subtrees. There is no rotation on a pivot outside U. Boundary attachment edges can change and are retained in the full ledger.

Thus counting rotations through this smaller ordinary BST is legitimate. Merely restricting to K would not have this property: external S ancestors can lie on its actual paths.

### 5.2 An independently defined short-scan surcharge

For an increasing fragment write a=min K, b=max K, and let d be the initial physical edge depth of a. Define the forward exterior frontier

\[
F^+=\{u\in U:u>b\},\qquad f^+=|F^+|,
\qquad \mathcal B(\omega)=d/2+C_{seq}f^+.
\tag{5.2}
\]

\[

\[
\boxed{

\tag{5.3}

    E_1=2(-,3),

## 8. Interrupted, reversing and repeated scan analysis

**Several proposed scans at one initial B state.** These are alternative comparisons unless they occur successively in W. Their alternative initial paths cannot be added as original work. Actual successive fragments use their actual different initial trees. If a repeated comparison really starts from the same complete pair, W must contain its full intervening return or be at a genuine paired-closed cut.

**Interruption and resumption.** DELETE does not interrupt B's scan geometry, even when it does substantial A preparation. A repeated current-root KEEP is also B identity. A KEEP to another rank can change the frontier, carry middle subtrees or destroy old runs. The later resumed fragment starts from that resulting actual tree. B work is saved when a rigid component was carried intact; it is repeated only when actual new operations reconstruct the relevant path. Neither outcome follows from the word “restart.”

**Reversals.** Every different-target reversal has its one genuine cyclic KEEP-gap A witness. There is no proved lower bound proportional to all nested histories affected. For example, alternating two adjacent root/child keys can use only zigs in both identical trees, with Q_B=0 and one A unit per transition. Large rank distance also does not imply large physical depth: an extreme root can have the opposite extreme as its child, with all intermediate keys in their middle component. Alternating those two keys uses depth-one accesses and transports that whole component. Rank distance, path depth and historical multiplicity must be separated.

**Distant repeated moves.** If a target is deep in the actual A tree, its original cost is counted. If it is deep only in B, the missing cross-tree payment is precisely (7.2). No per-key or per-region inventory is minted when the same move affects many outputs.

**Renewal histories crossing fragments.** Assign an R_irr gap once, at its actual consuming h and parent double. Its earlier births, intermediate fragments and later renewed hit are dependency records. Distributing the same gap over those transitions without an additional exact identity would duplicate it. The three-slot bound gives R_irr<=3Q_B^irregular, with no multiplier for renewal generations.

**Cyclic return.** A full circle displayed from several cuts is still the same original period. Reusing it as one restoration budget per fragment gives load equal to the number of references at every original A event. It is not a summation proof. The canonical decomposition and every selector here use original IDs once.

Section 9 gives an explicit repeated short-scan mechanism with genuine complete-state returns. It demonstrates why a large interruption count alone does not produce amplification, without claiming that all irregular mechanisms have that form.
