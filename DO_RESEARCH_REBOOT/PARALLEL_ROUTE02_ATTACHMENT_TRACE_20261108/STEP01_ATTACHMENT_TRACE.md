## 4. Every B attachment change, including the atomic link

At c(d), I is directly under x. Until pi(d), its external B parent is always x or b. Before the first hit it is a rigid hanger under outside B accesses.

The x-to-b switch is exactly

    b(x(L,T),R)  -->  x(L,b(T,R)),   label_S(T)=I.      (T2)

It promotes x and makes I the producer-facing child of b. It creates

    K = I union {b} union label_S(R),

which excludes x. Its bit is neutral if K is A-absent and decreasing if K is A-present.

The reverse b-to-x switch is exactly

    x(L,b(T,R))  -->  b(x(L,T),R),   label_S(T)=I.      (T3)

It promotes b and creates

    C = label_S(L) union {x} union I,

which excludes b. Again its sign is determined by actual A membership.

These are the only parent changes retaining I. A rotation involving its parent and its own root removes I and is retirement instead. A rotation inside I can reroot it without changing its external parent.

Record the entire alternating parent word

    x --h_1--> b --k_1--> x --h_2--> b --k_2--> ... ,

not just the final switch. It starts at x. All switches precede the first-hit B phase: transporting I as the middle hanger of an x/b rotation is incompatible with an ordinary splay whose target is inside that middle hanger. At the end of the complete producer access, I has parent b, since B has root x and the full side of x contains b as well as I.

For an opposite-retired output the word ends at b; its last h is Step 01's globally injective last-switch witness. If that h is the designated birth of another neutral demand e, then

    producer(e)=x,   I strictly contained in I_e,   p_e=b.

This is the atomic chain link. It changes I's external parent without an A operation. Such links form the inherited nonbranching same-producer chains.

For a producer-retired output the word ends at x. There is at least one departure and subsequent return before the first hit. Its last reverse switch is a genuine original event, not an A blocker addition.
