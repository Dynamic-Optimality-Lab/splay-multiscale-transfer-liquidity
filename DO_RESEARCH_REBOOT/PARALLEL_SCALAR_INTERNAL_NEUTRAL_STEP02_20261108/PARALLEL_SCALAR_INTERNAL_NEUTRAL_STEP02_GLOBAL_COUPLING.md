## C. Main source-coupling theorem
### C1. Exact atomic link and A history

Use the right-output orientation; every statement has the literal order-reflected left-output version. Let d -> e be an original Step 01 link, with producer x and inner output I=[l,a]. Set

\[
x=\operatorname{pred}_S(l),\qquad b=\operatorname{succ}_S(a).
\]

Its last producer-to-opposite boundary switch is the original neutral B rotation

\[
h(d):\quad b(x(L,T),R)\longrightarrow x(L,b(T,R)),
\quad \operatorname{label}_S(T)=I.
\tag{3}
\]

It creates the designated output I_e of neutral e, with original root b, the same producer x, and I strictly contained in I_e. Both d and e have the opposite-boundary histories required by the chain annotation. An outer node may already be paid by a fixed comparison; it remains a valid source witness, not a second unpaid demand.

For the unpaid cases studied below assume, exactly as after the inherited N9/L5 exclusions, that I is **never A-acquired in its original live B run**. Write

\[
L_I(A): x\text{ lies below }\operatorname{LCA}_A(I),\qquad
R_I(A): b\text{ lies below }\operatorname{LCA}_A(I).
\]

I is A-absent exactly when L_I or R_I holds.

### 2.1 Absence before the producer's A access

I excludes x. Every proper A label removed by an ordinary splay of x contains x. Every new proper label introduced in that access persists until its end. These literal rotation facts imply:

> If a proper S label excluding x is absent after splaying x, it was absent throughout that A access, including before it.

Consequently a neutral designated output was not made A-absent merely by its later B creation: it is already absent throughout its producer's A phase. At its neutral B birth A is rooted at x, so L_I is false and R_I is true.

Before the producer's A phase there need not be a prescribed physical A parent or a free witness. Initial absence can have either canonical blocker. In particular the B root b is not automatically an A ancestor of x. Labels not yet in B do not satisfy a current-B blocker-erasure sign theorem by default.

### 2.2 Final direct-parent interval

Let J_d be the maximal actual fine-step interval immediately before h(d) during which I remains a B subtree and its B parent is x. At the end, A is rooted at x, because h is in the B phase of e's producer access. Hence

\[
L_I=0\quad\hbox{just before }h(d).
\tag{4}
\]

Call d **activated** if L_I is true at some A boundary in J_d. Denote the set of such inner-link demands by \(\mathcal A\). For d in this set let eta(d) be the LAST actual A rotation in J_d changing L_I from true to false.

This is a source-defined class; its definition does not assert a free unit. Existence of eta follows from activation, (4), and the fact that B steps do not change A's blocker predicate for the fixed interval I.

### 2.3 Same-access and different-access links

If d and e are produced in the SAME original B access, there is no A operation between their creation/switch rotations. A stays rooted at x. Such a link is not activated.

If they are produced in DIFFERENT accesses, I has parent b at the END of d's complete producer access. Indeed B then has root x and its right child contains every S key greater than x, including b; that child cannot be the proper interval I excluding b. The only other possible parent is b.

Thus before a later h, I must undergo a reverse boundary switch. Let k(d) be the last one beginning J_d:

\[
k(d):\quad x(L,b(T,R))\longrightarrow b(x(L,T),R).
\tag{5}
\]

It creates the proper label

\[
C_d=\operatorname{label}_S(L)\cup\{x\}\cup I,
\quad x,I\subset C_d,\quad b\notin C_d.
\tag{6}
\]

The map d -> k(d) is injective on these different-access links: one physical rotation has one middle hanger, one current run and one original designated producer.

In an ordinary splay the promoted b in (5) is either the actual access target b or the intermediate parent in a first same-side rotation. In the latter case that access's target is strictly greater than b. No opposite-side or target-splay case is omitted.

## 3. A cost restriction for ALL different-access interior links

Let \(\mathcal L_{\rm cross}\) be the inner-link demands for which d and e have different producing accesses.

**Theorem 02.1.** At most two such links end in any one original B access, at most one per output side. Consequently

\[
|\mathcal L_{\rm cross}|\le 2K\le 2A(W),
\tag{7}
\]

where K counts the actual nontrivial B accesses that receive these links. There is an explicit injection into original A accounting units proving the second inequality, globally across all producers/chains.

**Proof.** In the receiving access to x, the inner I is an OLD B label, created before this access, and is a direct child of x just before its target-promotion switch h.

During an ordinary splay, rotations promoting an intermediate parent do not change x's children. A rotation promoting target x replaces the relevant child by its newly demoted pivot, with a NEW proper label. The former child becomes a hanger below that pivot. Target x is never demoted in its own access, so that old child cannot return as x's child later in the access.

Thus an old designated output can supply such an inner link only from x's original left or right child. There is one of each. Later same-side links in this access use outputs newly created here and are same-access links instead.

For each receiving nontrivial B access t, take the original operation gap after the immediately preceding KEEP through KEEP t, retaining every DELETE/helper. A begins at the preceding common A/B root g and ends rooted at x different from g. It contains an actual A StepEv. These gaps are disjoint for different t. Assign the at-most-two cross-link demands to the two original units of its first A StepEv. Different gaps use different physical event IDs. QED.

This is a genuine bounded-multiplicity association to counted A work. Its bits may be positive, occupied or virtual. It is NOT an additional unused pool to add to g1. The stronger theorem below obtains actual quiet units on a source-recognizable subclass.

## 4. Principal chain-interior theorem: globally distinct quiet A work

### 4.1 Direct-child erasure has exactly zero sign

**Lemma 02.2.** Fix any original A rotation with B fixed. Suppose the current B interval I is the right child subtree of producer x=pred_S(min I), I is not A-acquired, and the rotation erases L_I. Then the rotation is quiet and has sign exactly zero for the original Psi.

**Proof, retaining private operands.** The ordinary ancestor-removal identity forces a RIGHT rotation

\[
u(v(L,H),R)\longrightarrow v(L,u(H,R)),
\qquad x\le v<l\le u\le a.
\tag{8}
\]

The old promoted-child label contains x but excludes the S keys of I at or beyond u. It is not a current B label: every B label containing x contains the WHOLE I.

The new demoted-pivot label contains the whole I and excludes x. In B, the only label containing I while excluding its direct parent x is I itself. If the new label were common with B, I would become A-present, contrary to the hypothesis. Thus neither changed label can produce a loss or gain of a current B label. SET duplicates do not alter this exclusion. Delta Psi is zero.

A right rotation can only add right canonical flags. If one were added to a current B interval J, its span would contain v while max J<u. If v=x, J contains x. If x<v<l, there is no S key between x and l, so an S interval whose span contains v must again contain x. Therefore J contains I, giving max J>=a>=u, a contradiction. No canonical flag is added: the rotation is quiet. Reflection proves the left-output case. QED.

The unacquired-output hypothesis is essential. If the new label equals I, the erasure can be negative. That case belongs to the inherited acquisition mechanisms, not this zero-work theorem.

### 4.2 Global noncollision

**Theorem 02.3.** On \(\mathcal A\), the map

\[
d\longmapsto \operatorname{own}(\eta(d))
\tag{9}
\]

is a globally injective map into original quiet-zero A units. Hence

\[
\boxed{|\mathcal A|\le T_A,\qquad
A(\text{events containing these witnesses})\ge
\lceil|\mathcal A|/2\rceil.}
\tag{10}
\]

It requires no disjointness of preparation windows, sponsor runs or chains.

**Proof.** Lemma 02.2 gives each witness's exact zero sign and quietness.

At one right erasure (8), its promoted physical key v fixes the erased left boundary as x=floor_S(v). At that actual B snapshot x has only one right child. Hence the direct-child interval I, its current B run and its designated producing demand are uniquely determined. Two different right-output demands cannot select this same rotation. Left-output witnesses are left rotations and cannot collide with right-output witnesses. Their reflected uniqueness proof uses x=ceil_S(v).

Two real rotations of one StepEv are distinct units; at most two selected witnesses can belong to that event. The source is finite and all witnesses retain their original IDs, including across the cyclic seam. QED.

### 4.3 Actual raw payment and terminal bonus

The quiet-zero units in (9) are unused by the exact inherited

g_{\rm pre}=g1+\mathrm{N9}+\mathrm{L5}+\mathrm{L6}+\mathrm{L7}.
\]

g1 and the head selections use positive units; negative-own selections have a different sign; L6's zero choices ADD a canonical flag and are not quiet. Thus every previously unpaid activated inner demand can be assigned its own eta bit while retaining every g_pre pair.

If eta(d) is terminal, its distinct virtual bit is also unused by g_pre. Different eta rotations give distinct terminal virtual units. With v_A equal to the number of such terminal witnesses,

\[
\mathcal Z=\{\operatorname{own}(\eta(d)):d\in\mathcal A\},
\quad
\mathcal V=\{\operatorname{virt}(\eta(d)):\eta(d)\text{ terminal}\}
\]

are disjoint original-unit sets, of total size

\[
|\mathcal Z\sqcup\mathcal V|=|\mathcal A|+v_A
\le T_A+V_A.
\tag{11}
\]

The own unit pays one inner demand. A terminal bonus is a DIFFERENT unit, available only once. This is an actual subinventory of the old T_A/V_A terms, not new accounting money.

## 5. Source conditions that FORCE activation

**Theorem 02.4.** Every different-access inner link is activated if its last reverse switch k(d) satisfies either:

1. k(d) decreases the existing Psi; or
2. the access containing k(d) actually targets the opposite boundary b.

**Proof.** If k decreases, its created C_d is A-present. Since C_d contains I and excludes b, the right b blocker of I is false at that A snapshot. I is nevertheless A-absent, so L_I is true. This snapshot lies just inside J_d.

If the actual target is b, A is rooted at b during that entire B phase, again making R_I false. Since I stays A-absent, L_I is true. Equation (4) then forces its later erasure. QED.

A decreasing B reattachment may be a payment slot. Its old positive lambda head is not taken. The NEW payment is the separately forced quiet A bit (9).

### 5.1 A second, counted A operation for decreasing reattachments

Let \(\mathcal D\) be the subclass with decreasing k. At k, C_d is both A- and B-present. By eta, I has L_I false but is still A-absent, so R_I is true. C_d, which contains I and excludes b, cannot remain an A label then.

Let mu_d be the first subsequent B removal of this C_d run. If it occurs before eta, A is already C_d-absent at its target-root snapshot. In either case C_d has a first A loss beta(d) after k and before or at the earlier relevant absence boundary. That loss occurs while B still holds C_d, and is positive.

Different k create different C_d runs. One positive A rotation loses one common label in one run. Therefore beta is injective, and its positive bits are disjoint from the zero eta bits. In particular

\[
\boxed{2|\mathcal D|\le P+T_A\le 2A,\qquad
|\mathcal D|\le A.}
\tag{12}
\]

This is TWO globally distinct actual A rotations per such link, not two freely usable donors. Beta may already be g1-owned; eta supplies the one proved raw payment.

By complete A closure, each lost C_d has a first actual A return. The standard loss/first-return uniqueness argument applies to these distinct losses too. Its sign and owner depend on the B run at return; closure alone supplies no further unused unit. This restoration obligation is retained, not used to pay the chain a second time.

### 5.2 Exact remaining different-access alternative

A cross-access link not in \(\mathcal A\) has L_I identically false throughout J_d. It follows that:

- k(d) is neutral;
- b is an intermediate parent in the FIRST same-side rotation of an access to z>b;
- R_I is true throughout J_d;
- no actual A access, or intermediate global A root, in J_d lies in the closed physical key interval [l,b].

For the last assertion, a root in [l,a] makes x a descendant of LCA_A(I), whereas a root in (a,b] makes b unable to lie below that LCA. Either contradicts L_I=0 and the continued absence of I.

Since the A root at k's phase is z>b and at h's phase is x<l, at least one original root-changing A rotation must jump from a root >b to one <l without entering [l,b]. That is a real counted A operation, but it can be shared by several such intervals and can be positive. No bounded multiplicity or unused sign for that jump is proved here.

The reflected alternative has z<b, forbids [b,a], and jumps in the opposite direction. This is a forward source restriction on the unhandled subclass, not an asserted realizability theorem for arbitrary jump diagrams.


### 5.3 A single source law covers interior and near-side work at the A-cost level

The erasure theorem has a useful dual which is stronger than merely classifying more B histories.

**Lemma 02.5 — producer-side addition.** If a current unacquired B output I is a direct child of its original producer, an A rotation ADDING that producer's canonical flag has exactly zero sign.

For the right orientation the rotation is LEFT,

\[
u(L,v(H,R))\longrightarrow v(u(L,H),R),
\qquad x\le u<l\le v\le a.
\]

All of I lies in the old v-subtree. Otherwise its A LCA would be above this frame and would already contain x, so its left flag could not be newly added. The old v label contains I and excludes x; the only possible current B label with those properties is I, which was A-absent. It is not common. The new demoted u label contains x and excludes ceil_S(v) in I; every B label containing x contains all of I. It is not a B label either. Thus the A rotation is zero. Reflection is literal.

It is nonquiet, because it adds the specified flag. This zero bit is raw-free under g1, but can already be L6's selected unit.

**Theorem 02.6 — global producer-child transition law.** Let \(\mathcal T\) be ANY set of distinct original neutral designated-output demands, with unacquired output runs, for which the producer flag changes at least once at an A rotation while that output is a direct B child of its original producer. Choose one such actual transition per demand by a fixed original occurrence order. Then

\[
\boxed{|\mathcal T|\le T_A.}
\tag{22}
\]

The chosen map is injective across ALL producers, both sides, equal labels in different runs, different chains and near-side demands.

For additions, the old pivot determines the boundary producer; for erasures, the promoted node determines it, by the adjacent-S floor/ceiling rules. The current B child then fixes one output run and its one producer demand. Two additions or two erasures therefore cannot collide. An addition cannot collide with an erasure: Lemma 02.2 proves that the erasure is quiet, while the addition is not. Each chosen event is a real zero rotation. This proves (22) directly, not by an assumed capacity inequality.

The theorem concerns actual A structural effects relative to B. It does not depend on C09's comparison being available. On the inherited residual partition's own scope it gives, for the members possessing these transitions,

\[
\sum_C\bigl(L_C^{\rm transition}
+\varepsilon_C^{\rm transition}\bigr)
+|P_{\rm free}^{\rm transition}|
\le T_A.
\tag{23}
\]

Distinct demands are counted once even when an endpoint and an inner chain share their consumer access. Equation (23) is NOT asserted for members with no such producer-child transition.

### 5.4 Derivation for attached and unattached near-side inputs

Take a producer-near-side right output I=[l,a], original B root p, actual first target y<p, and actual retirement pivot x. Before that B access, I is a rigid subtree. During its first hit, rotations inside I can change its root without changing its external parent. Changing the external parent while retaining I would transport the whole I as the middle hanger of an x/b rotation; such a rotation cannot occur in an ordinary splay whose target lies INSIDE that middle hanger. Hence I is already the direct right child of x before the consumer's B phase, and B stays fixed throughout its A phase.

After A splays y in I, L_I is true. Thus if L_I was false earlier in this final producer-attached interval, at least one actual 0-to-1 A transition is compulsory. Lemma 02.5 makes it zero, and Theorem 02.6 controls its global multiplicity. This reasoning does not need y>=p, a common B1 companion or BND.

For an attached endpoint the D3 co-consumption rule gives y inside the preceding inner output. It still supplies one compulsory addition only if the stated earlier false flag exists. Sharing that A phase does not duplicate a rotation; (22) separates all qualifying claims. For an unattached near-side demand the same proof uses its ORIGINAL producer, current child and actual first target. No substitute predecessor is invented.

If the flag is already true at entry and remains true until retirement, the argument supplies no addition. In particular a B reattachment can move a pre-existing A blocker into the producer-child relation without changing A. For a decreasing reattachment its common C label must later be A-lost before the first hit, giving a positive rotation by the same actual-run argument as Section 5.1. The missing zero unit does not follow from that positive work. This is the exact failed implication of the universal near-side-addition candidate.

### 5.5 An occupied zero addition has a precise smaller owner

Suppose an actually unpaid near-side input p selects such an addition alpha while its B interval I_p is directly under x, and g_pre occupies own(alpha).

The owner is necessarily the L6 zero selection q at alpha. Every other g_pre type uses positive heads or negative own units. The added producer boundary is x, so q's designated interval K begins at l=succ_S(x) and contains the promoted A node in its span.

At this B snapshot I_p is the direct right child of x. Every current B interval with this minimum and without x is I_p or a proper prefix of it. Equality would identify the same current run and producer demand p, contrary to p being unpaid. Therefore

\[
I_q=K\subsetneq I_p,\qquad x_q=x_p.
\tag{24}
\]

The suffix statement reflects. Distinct occupied additions have distinct actual q owners.

For the exact g_pre comparison, a walk through such occupied additions consequently descends strictly through same-producer output labels. If a reached q has an independently selected producer-child transition, continue along that real zero unit; if it has an available recognized scan witness, use that witness. Quiet erasures and the proved scan pool are genuine free termini relative to g_pre. A first undefined owner is an actual producer-silent, unrecognized output. Strict inclusion bounds the number of these PARTICULAR owner steps by |S|-1; it does not create an unused endpoint there.

This is a proved source rank for these L6 edges, not an assertion that N8, arbitrary D4 edges and all frozen effective-owner edges share one rank. Later raw comparisons can own different zero bits, and require their actual source coverage separately.

### 5.6 What complete cyclic history does and does not force

For a designated output, A absence is already present before its producer's A phase. If it remains A-absent during its live B run, paired closure does not by itself require an A acquisition of that output: the initial and final A membership may both be zero. B can create and retire the label while A's membership never changes. Closure of the complete labelled A tree is stronger than this one membership, but no added per-label acquisition follows from it.

The persistent-blocker CCR histories preserve this distinction; they are not replaced by an acyclic missing-label graph. Likewise a first A return after a positive loss exists, but its own bit or head can already be owned. The positive beta and zero eta proven above are actual operations with proved multiplicities; no further expensive return is inferred.

Thus the new quantitative global restriction is (22), with the explicit counterclass of producer-silent output histories and the special family work in Section 7. It is not called a universal conservation theorem.
