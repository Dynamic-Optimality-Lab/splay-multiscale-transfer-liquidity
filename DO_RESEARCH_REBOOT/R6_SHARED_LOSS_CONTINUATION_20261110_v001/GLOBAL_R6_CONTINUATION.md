## 3. A universal two-channel event lemma, including regeneration signs

A left rotation g(L,p(H,R))->p(g(L,H),R) loses precisely the strict ancestor pairs (g,v), v in {p} union R, and gains (p,v), v in {g} union L. Its right reflection loses (g,v), v in {p} union L, and gains (p,v), v in {g} union R. All other ancestor pairs, including the external parent's, are unchanged. Hence a relation u->v can be destroyed only by an ACTUAL rotation demoting u.

For each fragment use its original A preparation span: the complete operation gaps after the preceding KEEP through every owned KEEP, including all incoming DELETEs and identity repeats. These spans are disjoint across fragments.

A path ticket has v=first target and weight 1/2; its preparation prefix ends at that first target's post-A boundary. A frontier ticket has v=last target and weight C; its span ends at the last target's post-A boundary. Both end with v the actual A root, so u->v is false there.

**A channel.** If u->v is A-present at ANY snapshot in that ticket's preparation prefix, select its first real A loss in that prefix. This also covers initially absent relations that are acquired and then lost during actual preparation. Existence follows from the ending root hit, not from a B rotation.

The demoted key fixes u. A fragment has at most one ticket with that u in each role. Disjoint original operation spans prevent another fragment from using the same A bit. Therefore the selector has globally at most ONE reference per role, at most TWO unweighted references and weighted load <=w per real A rotation.

**B-decrease channel.** For a ticket not covered by the A channel, take the first actual B loss of u->v in its owned B span. It exists because the appropriate target becomes B root. If that selected bit decreases Psi, charge this channel to that exact original decreasing bit. The same demoted-key and disjoint-span argument gives load <=ONE per role, weighted load <=w per real decreasing B bit.

These are universal arguments for every admissible W, both orientations, singleton fragments, roots, empty wings, private A nodes and cyclic seams. Neither requires a new A rotation inside each neutral B double. They do not claim coverage of every ticket.

Partition the retained tickets in that order: A channel, then B-decrease channel, then those left with A absence throughout their preparation prefix AND a neutral first B loss. Let N(W) be the weighted sum in this LAST DIAGNOSTIC INVENTORY. This is not a new residual stage or a success criterion. The exact ticket partition and proved global loads give

\[
\mathcal F_6^\partial
 \le wR_A+wD_B+N
 \le(4C+2)A+N,                                      \tag{3.1}
\]
\[
\boxed{R_6\le(15C+6)A+3N.}                           \tag{3.2}
\]

All load coefficients are explicit. A StepEv has at most two real bits; a physical bit is never represented as two distinct resources. These loads can overlap older payments and each other through the SAME A inventory. Their coefficients are added, not treated as vacant units. No g1 owner or release is reassigned, and negative/zero A signs are not silently positive credit.

Equation (3.2) is NOT the requested absolute bound. Indeed \(N\le\mathcal F_6^\partial\le\mathcal F_B\), and the previous report proves \(\mathcal F_B\le (1+2C)Q_B+wA\). Together with (1.1) and (3.2), a uniform N/A bound is equivalent up to constants to the all-R6 target. Introducing N does not reduce the open difficulty or constitute net scalar progress.

The point of the lemma is narrower: it proves exactly what actual events and global multiplicities are already justified, so that the remaining coverage cannot be supplied by an unstated implication.
