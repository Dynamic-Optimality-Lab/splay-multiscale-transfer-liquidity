## 2. Arbitrary A has a finite branch quotient

Prune only mark-free subtrees of A, then contract ALL unmarked unary
vertices of its marked ancestor core. Keep every marked vertex and every
unmarked branching vertex. At a natural boundary the root is marked.

There are at most4 unmarked branchers: in the resulting binary tree the
number of branching vertices is leaves-1, all leaves are marked, and a
marked root with six marks is not a leaf. If it is unary there are at
most5 leaves; if it branches it itself occupies one branching place.
Either way at most4 branching places can be unmarked.

No two unmarked branchers occupy the same adjacent-mark order gap. If
one is an ancestor of the other, the latter's child towards the former
contains a mark strictly between them. If neither is an ancestor, their
LCA's separating child bounds imply that both cannot have marks on both
sides inside an interval without a mark. Equivalently, each brancher is
the unique LCA of the two neighboring marked order intervals it separates.
