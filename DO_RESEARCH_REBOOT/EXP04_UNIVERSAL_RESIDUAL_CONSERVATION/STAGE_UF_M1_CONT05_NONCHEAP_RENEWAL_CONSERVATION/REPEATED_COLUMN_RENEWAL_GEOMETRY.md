# RN-B: filtered renewal geometry on arbitrary original sources

Keep three predecessors distinct: previous ALL-double same-g use w; previous physical INDEX-noncheap use; and RN-A's previous ORIGINAL C_old parent u. They need not agree. Only w is the original M0 C predecessor. The filtered renewal graph may be cyclic when there is no A reset; it is not the M0 forest.

Fix an RN-A edge u->v in one A-demotion epoch or its no-reset cyclic case. The interval is AFTER the FULL B phase of u through the pre-B/post-A boundary of v. There is no actual A demotion of g in it.

## A rigidity

No request in this interval can target g or a strict descendant of its complete A component. An inside request would demote g. Targeting g would leave A rooted there and the first later different request, at latest v, would demote it. A non-target A promotion of g is followed by its demotion in the second SS bit. Thus the complete A subtree at g has the same keys and internal edges throughout; outside requests can move its external attachment.

This does not freeze all A SET labels at other roots. Acquisition tests for original F/J and opposite siblings remain the original Pi/SET predicates.

## Exhaustive reset-free B alternatives

Let the original target at v be x_v.

FREE: no B primitive promotes g in the interval. Only a demotion of g can alter its rooted B descendant set, and such a change only shrinks it. Thus, on FULL physical sets as well as S labels,
\[
F_v\subseteq J_g(u)\subsetneq F_u.
\]
The relation g ancestor-of x_v holds continuously from post-B(u) to v, because x_v remains in the final set and a lost descendant cannot return without promotion of g. Internal path edges may nevertheless be destroyed and recreated. No unchanged-edge claim follows.

MEDIATED: at least one B primitive promotes g. It cannot target g by the A rigidity argument. Non-target promotion is exactly bit0 of an LL/RR double r with old parent g. Choose the LAST such primitive beta. After BOTH bits its completed old-parent output is P_g(r), and thereafter the g component only shrinks:
\[
F_v\subseteq P_g(r)\subsetneq F_r.
\]
The intermediate bit0 frame still contains r's target and is not a valid completed output.

The first all-double old-g use w0 after beta is an original P vertex. Later old-g uses through v have type C since no later promotion/reset occurs. If v is an actual S_UU C endpoint, w0 precedes v; it cannot be identified with the filtered u or with the whole-frame U birth creator without checking.

These two cases are exhaustive. A reset between consecutive physical noncheap uses starts a different epoch and is handled by the first-use mechanism instead; it is not an RN-A surviving edge.

## Additional demotions and original births

Intervening LR/RL old-parent demotions can shrink g without promoting it. For the eventual U C consumer, its actual whole-frame creator is either the immediate same-g predecessor's chosen J (G), or an intervening opposite double's old-parent output P (O). The filtered u can be much earlier. Full Pi, creator U and unacquired output conditions remain mandatory.

A terminal B zig at g would require a previous KEEP targeting g and hence an A reset before v, so it cannot occur inside this reset-free interval. Terminal zigs at other roots can transport the component. Private off-path keys are retained; none of these statements uses a projected ancestry or virtual bit.

## Unique-target loss

Between promotions of g, each old-g double removes its own marked target from the g component. Those removed targets are distinct until a new promotion permits growth. This gives a frame-size bound on one block, not an absolute A budget. Different g columns can lose the same target at one access, and arbitrarily long blocks exist.
