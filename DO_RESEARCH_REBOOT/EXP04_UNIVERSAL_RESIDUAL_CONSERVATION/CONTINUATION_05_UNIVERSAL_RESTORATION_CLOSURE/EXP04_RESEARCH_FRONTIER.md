# First unresolved implication

**Principal outcome C: a new physical obstruction to local
restoration accounting.** No genuine post-hybrid overflow
has been certified; the original aggregate theorem remains open.

The primary target is the unchanged \(\sum_h e_h\le KA\).
All historical definitions and the frozen coefficient
\(47+48C_{\rm seq}+102C_{\rm DF}\) survive. The auxiliary
last-promotion payment deletes only literal overflow gaps
at its proved paid parents, leaving the exact \(R_5\).

The first unproved implication is an absolute joint A bound
on the two remaining classes:

1. An unpromoted grandparent survives below B and above its
   later target throughout its own cyclic interval, while
   its complete A component remains rigid. Many such
   intervals can end at the same KEEP. A single fixed
   column is already paid; their superposition is the issue.
2. A distinct non-target B promotion from a same-side
   restorer is selected by the later original parent. The
   restorer is beyond its original prefix, not protected
   by an eligible old comparison, fully neutral, and
   neither whole-frame nor completed-parent-output
   exposure supplies a real A bit. Its old grandparent
   may not be a vertex of the fixed residual pool.

The last-promotion forest avoids cyclic self-funding. For the
direct route on all B doubles there are no external roots:
every root uses a distinct A demotion. The genuinely missing
bound is the aggregate size of that forest after accounting
for its branching and temporal overlap. Individual components
need not have bounded height. Two children can have overlapping
frames. Even consecutive KEEPs can carry arbitrarily many
retention edges sharing one actual A StepEv. Consequently a
valid A resource law must account for work outside those
causal spans or prove a quantitative exclusion specific to
the frozen post-hybrid predicates.

**Next concrete proof test:** follow the actual selected
restorer's whole-frame B run and its completed old-parent
output run, through all intervening A/B phases and first
hits. Determine whether a wide family of their *useful*
receivers forces original work when those runs are put
together, without assuming disjointness. A raw count of
marked descendant gains is ruled out by C05-M. Counting
selected donor occurrences avoids that obstruction but
does not yet price them. For retained opposite outputs,
the rigid-suffix orientation law gives a transition to
same-side or an actual B disturbance; it is still missing
a globally bounded way to pay the disturbances and
repeated branches. Do not assume the missing statement
from exact closure or strict inclusion alone.

**Adversarial target:** construct simultaneously many such
useful selected receivers AFTER all fixed deductions.
The large opposite path and the upper silent restorer
are physical building blocks, not surviving tail samples.
Appending a diagonal support return to a small active
period is erasable by the old guard rule and cannot
certify a hybrid escape. In the new large 516-key example
even the raw periodic case is already positive. A
future real escape needs all periodic cuts/variants
and all eligible local arcs rejected, plus full original
event and Pi/cohort verification. Do not use a large
prehybrid packet or a mass of unused gains as a substitute.

The direct alternative accepts a proof on arbitrary finite
fragments with absolute multiplier and finite tree-only
overhead. The new O(n) restoration is an exact reduction,
not a proof of that multiplier. The downstream implication
is conditionally audited; it cannot be activated until
one of the two equivalent existence problems is proved.
