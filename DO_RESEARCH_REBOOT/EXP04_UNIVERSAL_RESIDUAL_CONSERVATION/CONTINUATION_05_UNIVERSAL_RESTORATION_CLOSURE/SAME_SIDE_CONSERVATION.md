# Same-side restoration: exact physical laws

Use X for the current target-rooted subtree in one double. For LL the
grandparent G has left child P, P has left child X and right middle
component M. Let R be G's old right subtree. First promote P over G;
then promote x over P. The completed G output contains G, M and R.
The completed P output contains P, that whole G output, and the old
right component of X. All lower completed outputs were inside X
before the double. Thus the G output is disjoint from every lower
grandparent output. The mirror for RR exchanges left and right.

These descriptions include the real middle subtrees; nothing is
contracted. A later step of this same access rotates only on the
remaining route above x. It can transport a completed off-path
subtree intact but does not change its internal keys or edges.
In particular all completed same-side grandparent outputs in an
access are pairwise disjoint, even with intervening opposite-side
doubles. This reproves C04 P7 at complete-state scope.

The non-target promotion of P in bit 0 gains exactly
\[
\{G\}\cup R\quad\hbox{(LL)}
\]
as descendants, and the reflected opposite-side hanger for RR.
All these gains persist in the completed P output after bit 1 and
the whole splay. The middle M was already in P. This is a statement
about actual descendant sets before and after the same physical
step, not a count of new A resources. It is the identity used in the
raw marked restoration mass counterfamily.

If k same-side old grandparents recur together at a later target y,
at most one of their initial completed outputs contains y. At least
k−1 of the relations must therefore have been gained by distinct B
primitives promoting their different old grandparent keys. If y is
the earlier target, none of those outputs contains it. More generally,
for a specified mixed packet use its exact completed-output membership
count \(r(y)\); at least \(k-r(y)\) gains are forced for the k keys
that recur. Repeated losses after an initially retained relation can
force more gains. Exact closure supplies the same argument across a
cyclic seam.

A required promotion either is a B target access to that key (then
a real A demotion is forced by the next distinct later target), or
is a same-side old-parent promotion. Multiple different descendant
gains at one non-target promotion are not multiple donors. Selecting
the last relevant gain or promotion per cyclic column interval makes
these actual B donors nonrepeating across the whole period. The signed
SET and A exposure payments apply to this selected set, not to all
gained pairs indiscriminately.

**What is still open:** a globally bounded payment for silent, noncheap

recreated/retained ancestry controls are not overridden.
