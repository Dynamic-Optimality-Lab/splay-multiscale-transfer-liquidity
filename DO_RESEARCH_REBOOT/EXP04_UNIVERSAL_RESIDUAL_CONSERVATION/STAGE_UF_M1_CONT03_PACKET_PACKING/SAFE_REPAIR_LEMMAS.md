# Concrete physical repair; no assumed avoidance oracle

The split-vine construction suffices for the packing refutation. A second source generator tests whether that refutation depends on forbidding every internal A label.

## Safe shallow access

In any complete physical BST, a splay whose target has depth<=2 creates only full physical prefixes or suffixes at its demoted outputs.

- Zig: the old root's new subtree is the prefix/suffix on one side of the target.
- RR: both demoted outputs are prefixes; LL reflects them to suffixes.
- LR: old-parent output is a prefix and old-grandparent output a suffix.
- RL: old-parent output is a suffix and old-grandparent output a prefix.

This is the literal two-bit table at the full tree root. Restricting any nonempty prefix/suffix to fixed S includes an S extreme. Thus no shallow access creates a nonempty internal S label, even with private keys. Any previously absent finite forbidden collection of internal labels stays absent.

## Determined protected-target procedure

At each requested B target x, trial the ordinary A splay, checking every real primitive label. If it would create a protected label, choose the depth-two ancestor y on the actual A search path to x and execute a measured DELETE y. That access is safe by the preceding lemma.

It strictly decreases x's physical depth. In an SS case, descendants of y either retain their depth below y or acquire one additional ancestor from the transferred middle; the whole y-to-root distance falls by2. In an opposite case each descendant lies in one transferred side and acquires at most one additional ancestor. Thus the net decrease is at least1 in every case. Unsafe x cannot already have depth<=2. The procedure terminates and then executes the original KEEP.

All simulated rejected trials are computation only. Every emitted helper is actual original A work. This generator is not asserted to preserve the old source's A budget.
