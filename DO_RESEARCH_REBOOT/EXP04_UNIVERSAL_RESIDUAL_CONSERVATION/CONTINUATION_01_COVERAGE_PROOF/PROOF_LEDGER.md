## C1. Cyclic interval subcover — PROVED, finite constructive lemma

For any finite family of cyclic intervals of at most one period, repeatedly
remove any interval whose operation positions are already covered by the
others. Deletion terminates and preserves the union. The remaining cover is
irredundant and has point multiplicity at most two.

Proof: a full-circle interval alone covers everything; if it remains, all
others would be redundant. Otherwise suppose three remaining arcs share
position p. Lift them to line intervals containing the same lift of p. If
one contains another, the contained arc is redundant. Otherwise order their
left endpoints increasingly; their right endpoints then also increase.
The middle interval lies in the union of the first and third, since both
contain p. This again contradicts irredundancy. Endpoints are discrete
original operation positions, so no boundary-credit convention is hidden.

There is a deterministic finite construction: start with all eligible arcs,
and remove redundant arcs in reverse lexicographic order. An arc that was
not redundant has a private position; later removals cannot eliminate that
private witness. One pass therefore suffices. No matching/existence premise,
laminar-depth bound or finite-corpus assumption is used.
