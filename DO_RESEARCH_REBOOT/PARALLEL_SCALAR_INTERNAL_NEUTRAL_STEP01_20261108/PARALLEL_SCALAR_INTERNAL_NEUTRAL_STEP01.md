### D1. Injective last boundary switch

**Theorem.** For each d in O there is a unique last B rotation h(d), strictly after creation of its designated output I_d and before its retirement, changing its parent from producer to opposite boundary. The map d -> h(d) is globally injective.

For a right output I=[l,a], x=pred_S(l), b=succ_S(a), that switch is exactly (7), with label_S(T)=I. It creates

\[
K_h=I\cup\{b\}\cup\operatorname{label}_S(R),
\quad \min K_h=l,\quad \operatorname{root}_B(K_h)=b,
\quad I\subsetneq K_h.
\tag{8}
\]

A left output has the reflected identity and preserves its maximum.

**Proof.** At its designated creation the new output is a direct child of the access target, its producer: this is the second SS output or either zig-zag outer output. Before its first B hit it is a rigid subtree under every outside B access.

The parent of a proper B interval is its nearest S boundary on that parent's side. Thus I's parent is always x or b while its run persists. A rotation inside I may carry its whole label to a new root during the first-hit phase, but does not change its external parent. A rotation above its parent or in an unrelated hanger does not change that parent. Rotating its parent with I's root would REMOVE I and end the run.

The only remaining way to change the parent while retaining I is to transport the WHOLE I subtree as a rotation's middle hanger. Its two boundary operands must then be x,b. The x-to-b change is precisely (7).

At birth its parent is x. Immediately before an opposite-pivot retirement its parent is b. The finite actual run therefore has a last x-to-b transition. Such a switch cannot occur during an ordinary access INSIDE I: its promoted operand is x outside I, whereas the target lies in the middle hanger; an SS parent promotion requires the target on the other, same-side branch. A target promotion also cannot promote x in that access. Hence h precedes the consumer's B phase.

At one physical rotation there is only one middle hanger. If it is nonempty in S, it has one current B-label run and at most one original designated producer. Consequently two different d cannot name the same h. Reading the new demoted b-subtree proves (8). No A bit is assigned by this argument. QED.

Thus a specified B rotation supports at most ONE opposite history through this last-switch relation; one B double supports at most TWO. This is a global bound, not a bound per producer or per cached source.

### D2. Designated-output continuation has no branching and keeps its producer

If K_h is the designated output of another actual neutral double e, then:

\[
x_e=x_d,\quad
I_d\subsetneq I_e,\quad
\text{same output side},\quad
p_e=b_d.
\tag{9}
\]

At e's output creation I_d is exactly the child of p_e on the producer-facing side.

**Proof.** The promoted operand in h is x_d. In an ordinary B splay it is either the actual target or the intermediate parent in the FIRST SS rotation. In the intermediate-parent case K_h is the INNER first SS output, which is not a designated outer output. In every other double case the promoted operand is the actual target, so a neutral e designating K_h has target x_d. Equation (8) fixes the output side, containment and root; (7) fixes the child attachment. The reflected argument is identical. QED.

Put an edge d -> e only when e also belongs to the chosen O and its designated creation is h(d). Every node has outdegree at most one. Every node has indegree at most one: e has one designated creation and D1 permits only one predecessor at that rotation. Along every edge designated labels strictly increase.

Therefore this graph is a disjoint union of **finite directed chains**, not a branching forest or a source cycle. All nodes on a chain have one producer and one side. For right outputs their maxima strictly increase and each next original root is the preceding opposite boundary; for left outputs minima strictly decrease.

With \(\mathscr C\) the chain set, E its link set and lengths L_C,

\[
|O|=\sum_{C\in\mathscr C}L_C,\qquad
|\mathscr C|=|O|-|E|.
\tag{10}
\]

Terminal switches h(last(C)) are distinct. A right chain with producer x has length at most |{s in S:s>x}|-1, since every node still has an opposite neighbor. The reflected bound holds on the left. This bound grows with support; it is not a constant carry bound.

Unlike C02's whole-frame forest, these links concern CREATED OUTPUT labels and a specified last external-parent change. C02 allows two children. Here both in/out degrees are at most one and the producer is constant. This is the new overlap restriction.

### D3. Exact first-consumer rule and attachment of near-side inputs

For a link d -> e, let t_d,t_e be the actual first-hit accesses of their output runs, ordered in the causal interval after e's birth. For a right chain,

\[
t_e\le t_d,\qquad
t_e=t_d\ \Longleftrightarrow\ y_e<p_e.
\tag{11}
\]

For a left chain replace < by >.

Indeed I_d is the complete left child subtree of p_e at birth of I_e. Before the first hit of I_e all that internal geometry is rigid. Its target is in I_d exactly when it is below p_e. If so the same access first hits both runs. Otherwise it first hits I_e outside I_d, which survives for a later access. This permits many chain nodes to retire in ONE access; they are not distinct A operations.

A useful converse holds with exact provenance. Suppose a producer-near-side input e has, at its designated birth, a producer-facing child whose live label is another designated neutral output I_d with the SAME original producer. If d is still a residual claim, then d is opposite-boundary and h(d) is e's designated creation.

To prove this, e's creation transports that child from producer x to p_e, and creates I_e. The near-side first target lies in the child. C09's literal first-hit/inside-pivot lemma, or its four-rotation proof, makes the child's retirement pivot lie inside I_e. Its only boundaries are x outside I_e and p_e inside it. The pivot must be p_e, its opposite boundary. No outside access can change this child attachment before that first hit. Thus this is the last departure and the two claims share their first consumer.

This pairs such near-side endpoints injectively with preceding opposite chains. A near-side input has only one such child and one designated creation. A child created by its OTHER boundary, an already-paid child, or a child with no designated neutral provenance does not satisfy this converse. Those cases remain attached to their actual source roles.
