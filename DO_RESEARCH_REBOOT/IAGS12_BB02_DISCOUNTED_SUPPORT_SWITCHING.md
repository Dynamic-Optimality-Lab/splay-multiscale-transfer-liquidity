## 16. Final recurrence and benchmark-limit calculation, if justified

The following is a **CONDITIONAL implication**, reconstructed independently. Suppose genuine auxiliaries, a source-defined partition and admissible potentials satisfy BB-SWITCH on every source with absolute nonnegative c,beta,kappa, integer d>=2, n0, and \(\theta=\beta\kappa<1\). \[
 Q_B(W)\le aA(W)+\beta\sum_i\lambda_iR_6(W_i),
\]
and, since \(R_6(W)\le Q_B(W)\), the same upper bound holds for R6(W).

\[
 R_6(W)/A(W)\le a+\beta F_6(n^d)
                \frac{\sum_i\lambda_iA(W_i)}{A(W)}
 \le a+\theta F_6(n^d).
\]

Taking a supremum, without assuming attainment, gives

\[
 F_6(n)\le a+\theta F_6(n^d).
\]

Iteration to FINITE depth k retains the remainder:

\[
 F_6(n)\le a\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\theta^j
              +\theta^kF_6(n^{d^k}).

For fixed n>=2 set \(L=\log_2n\). Once smoothing is inactive,

\[
 u_k=\log_2\log_2(n^{d^k})
     =k\log_2d+\log_2L,\qquad
 g(n^{d^k})=u_k(1+\log_2u_k)^2
          =O_{n,d}(k(\log(k+2))^2).
\]

For0<theta<1 the exponential \(\theta^k\) dominates that polynomial-logarithmic factor, so the whole terminal term tends to zero. Theta=0 is immediate. Therefore

\[
 F_6(n)\le\frac{2+c}{1-\theta}.
 \tag{16.1 — CONDITIONAL}

Monotonicity gives the same bound at n<N0 by comparison with F6(N0); alternatively the smoothed benchmark gives an absolute finite maximum over those sizes. There is no interchange of a supremum and a limit or infinite physical source.

In fact, if BB-SWITCH itself holds on all sources, its original DIRECT Q inequality and (16.1) allow the sharper
\[
 Q_B(W)\le aA+\theta\frac a{1-\theta}A
         =\frac{2+c}{1-\theta}A.
 \tag{16.2 — CONDITIONAL}
\]
The larger D6 coefficient remains a valid implication. Neither is an established URCD coefficient: c and theta for the one-step source theorem are missing.

Polynomial population matters: the ceiling must satisfy
\(\theta^kg(n_k)\to0\) along the actual enlargement orbit. Theta=1 gives no vanishing term from this benchmark; faster growth can defeat a fixed discount. The same-universe erasure candidate has an even simpler conditional subtraction (§11), but still lacks its physical comparison.
