Reflect for the upper direction. This verifies that every arc in the new graph has a real terminal receipt with the same original meaning.

There is a joint restriction on a producer whose lower parent-repeat arc and either right-hand arc lie in the same cut. Work in the LL orientation with old pivots $p<g$. Let $\tau_L$ be the actual successor at $p$ with target $y_L<p$, and let $\kappa_R$ be the first genuine hit of its original parked interval $J=[p+1,U_B(g)]$. The other arc is either the same-gain repeat at $g$, with target strictly in $(p,g)$, or the used upper output at $p$. Both require a hit in $J$. Until $\tau_L$, the same lower gain at $p$ continuously holds $y_L$. Until $\kappa_R$, the entire $J$ stays a literal B subtree.

There is at most one such producer for a fixed lifted pair $(\tau_L,\kappa_R)$ in an orientation. For two alleged producers, observe the phase boundary after the later birth and before both destinations. Their two intervals are pending and both contain the right hit's target, so they are nested. If $p_1<p_2$, this puts $p_2$ inside the outer interval starting at $p_1+1$. The subtree of $p_2$ could not then contain $y_L<p_1$, contradicting its live lower successor. Equal parents are ruled out by the unique original predecessor of the receiving loss arm. When folding one period there are at most two possible relative windings for any distinct pair of genuine endpoint IDs, since each destination is less than one period after its source. In particular this is an event-identity bound, not an assumption of unit load on either of the two genuine endpoints.

### Where the proof stops

The exact flow allows alternatives from all four old cases in the same optimization. It removes the requirement to prove each selected arm's residual in isolation. The branch-pair uniqueness just proved does not control the aggregate number of different pairs. The already verified many-column shared-hit construction contains many distinct such pairs over a short low-pass and return segment, with earlier actual paid support outside that segment. This is why the next bound must use nonlocal original A work or a global structural cancellation paired with that work. ## Direct A recharge test across nested subtree intervals

2026-11-24. The joint cancellation calculation does not prove that original A work limits how much usable B ancestry can be rebuilt. The following test uses the actual A rotations and checks the aggregate accounting on one complete source at all nested scales. Case 1 and $\Omega_5=O(A)$ remain open.

Let $V(I)$ be the sum of the lengths of the left and right boundary spines of the current B tree projected to the contiguous interval $I$. Set $V(\varnothing)=0$. At a B phase its target is already A's root, so it is outside every proper A subtree interval. A physical rotation with an exterior pivot projects to the identity. A rotation with both pivots retained acts on the relevant boundary spine. If the demoted key is the projected root, one spine loses a key and the other gains a key. Otherwise the appropriate spine loses one key and the opposite spine is unchanged. This makes $V(I)$ nonincreasing during B for every current proper A subtree interval $I$.

Summing $V$ over all proper A subtree intervals overcharges even the unpaid work. Use the archived aligned family with the two low KEEPs $3,4$. Its complete work is $A=\Theta(h)$. At B KEEP 3 the proper A intervals $[j,h]$ exist for every $4\le j\le h$ and the internal B keys form a descending vine. For $\lceil h/8\rceil\le i\le\lfloor h/4\rfloor$, the first primitive of LL double $i$ promotes $p=2i+2$ over $g=2i+3<h$. For each $4\le j\le p$, both pivots lie in $[j,h]$ and $h$ is still a retained ancestor of $g$. Thus $V([j,h])$ decreases by one. Only an absolute number of these doubles are paid, by the archived low-segment bank calculation. The total decrease counted on the unpaid doubles alone is $\Omega(h^2)$. B never increases this sum and both trees close, so its signed A recharge over the complete period is also $\Omega(h^2)$. The underlying unpaid occurrence is one physical double. The multiplicity here comes from the proposed accounting formula. The complete aligned construction and bank calculation are in the repository's $\Omega_5$ geometric rectangles checkpoint.

There is an exact way to identify each such B transition once in the same physical trees. Write $I_v$ for the current A subtree interval at $v$ and $I_\ell,I_r$ for its two child intervals. Use the coordinate

$$
c_v=2+V(I_\ell)+V(I_r)-V(I_v).
$$

A real A primitive can change these coordinates only at its two pivots. The rotation frame as a whole keeps its interval, so every A vertex outside the two pivots keeps its own and its two child intervals. For a non-target B primitive let the affected proper intervals be those containing both B pivots and an ancestor of the demoted pivot. These form one upward segment of the A ancestor chain through the maximal proper interval on that side of the target. The B primitive increases $c_v$ by one at the bottom of that nonempty segment and changes no other proper coordinate. Target primitives leave all proper coordinates unchanged. There is at most one same-side double per orientation per KEEP for which the demoted pivot is already the root in the maximal proper projection, since there is then no higher old pivot on that side of the target. Those exceptions total at most $2K_B$.

For fixed absolute $k$, the first $k$ increases at each key between successive A pivots of that key therefore have budget $2kR_A$. A key untouched by A throughout the period cannot increase at all because its coordinate stays proper, never decreases and returns to its starting value. This counts all frame changes together and counts each recorded same-side B double once. It supplies no estimate on how many later increases can accumulate while a frame stays fixed, then be released when A moves its boundary. In particular, the per-rotation bound on the number of changed coordinates gives no bound on their combined change in value. A proof still needs a global original-A bound for that renewal across completed branches, displacements and companion restoration.
