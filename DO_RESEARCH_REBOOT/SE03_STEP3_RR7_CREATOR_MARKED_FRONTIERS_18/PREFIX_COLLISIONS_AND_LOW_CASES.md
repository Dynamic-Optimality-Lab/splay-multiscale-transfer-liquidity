# Exact prefix collisions and the surviving signed debt cases

For seven-key maximal sources, beta+z<=4. A creator StepEv's GLOBAL
event index j inside its original natural macro is the relevant index,
not its local event index within one DELETE. Let m=min(2H,beta+z).
Bit b of that event is in h_Lambda's image iff

    2(j-1)+b < m.

Thus j>=3 is protected automatically. At j1, bit0 collides iff m>=1,
bit1 iff m>=2. At j2, bit0 collides iff m>=3, bit1 iff m4. These are
equalities of physical source ordinals, not a chart representative rule.

The RR7 counter has a creator at macro event2, m3: its bit0 is spent,
bit1 is the protected mate. The marked-parent counter's creator is
macro event1 in an H2,m3 macro: BOTH bits of that event are spent; the
macro's LAST unit is protected. A universal same-event-mate rule is
therefore false at the marked-frontier source scope.

For r7, m4 can occur only at beta3,z1,u0,Q3: the three branching
ancestors, leaf target and three disjoint off-path marked witnesses
already use all7 marks. Its marked-parent skeleton is a four-leaf
comb, with one off-path leaf per ancestor. At H2 it has NO physical
comparison tail despite original price2H-Q=+1. It is an explicit
remaining saturated +1 producer class, not silently covered by the
two H2/Q2 examples. m3 with H>=2 requires beta2,z1 at r7; H1 can also
be physically saturated from larger beta+z.

mechanism. A signed transfer must carry its deficit to genuinely
additional source capacity; exact local bit patches alone are insufficient.
