## The critical logical distinction

Suppose an existing successful allocation is:

\[
a(p)=u_1.
\]

Suppose another physical bit \(u_2\) could also fund \(p\), while a new demand \(q\) can only use \(u_1\).

Then a matching may exist:

\[

\]

even though the frozen assignment:

\[

\]

cannot be extended.

This is only an abstract illustration.

It is NOT an original-source counterexample.

The actual campaign must determine whether genuine splay-source geometry excludes such conflicts or permits an analogous one.

The prescribed successful effective allocation admits a physical owner-preserving extension to all required new demands.

**Proposition M — Matching existence**

Proposition F is stronger than Proposition M when the alternative assignment is permitted only in a separate proof architecture.

Do not conflate them.
