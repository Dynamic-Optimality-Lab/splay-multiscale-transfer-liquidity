# Global, not merely pairwise, nonoverlap

For each marked key its Boolean leaf changes alternate cyclically. Mapping
each birth to the preceding death is a bijection of those two finite
occurrence sets. At one genuine macro only the accessed key can lose leaf
status. Thus all death-reserved unit IDs are distinct over the ENTIRE
family, regardless of group count. Local branch allocations exclude the
macro's reserved bit and lie in disjoint actual macro inventories.
These two arguments jointly prove injectivity of h_Lambda for arbitrarily
many groups, not a conclusion from singleton/pairwise tests or assumed Hall.

For fixed a, partial h_Lambda/a augmenting paths have unique backwards
predecessors. Two different missing roots cannot meet, including at an
undefined branch owner, and cannot enter a cycle. Free terminals and blocked
frontiers are therefore simultaneously distinct. Successful additions fix
EVERY old a value. This proves a partial collective theorem and identifies
exactly the unresolved frontier, not universal donor availability there.

In the explicit repeated-block family each block has<=4 branch slots and
its own physical reset supplies the selected repair units. The resets are
disjoint by original occurrence, and h_Lambda uses none of their units.
Consequently the full family repair is simultaneous for all finite block
counts. Source overlap of a supplier macro with the next block causes no
donor collision: ownership is tied to the previous block's reset events.

For arbitrary histories the existence of such spare source regions is
OPEN. No source-derived collective expansion inequality for the remaining
branch frontiers is asserted. Raw donor uniqueness does not establish
availability after frozen owners; the explicit r7 blocking path disproves
that shortcut under an actual certified six-key auxiliary map.
