## 2. What one actual B-neutral double does

|---|---|---|---|---|

| LR: g(p(T0,x(T1,T2)),T3) | {x}+U1+U2 | {p}+U0+U1 | {p,x}+U0+U1+U2 | {g}+U2+U3 |
| RL: g(T0,p(x(T1,T2),T3)) | {x}+U1+U2 | {p}+U2+U3 | {p,x}+U1+U2+U3 | {g}+U0+U1 |

Each original fine step p=(t,k,b) removes I_p and creates J_p.

    DeltaPsi_B(p)=-1+1_{J_p absent from J_S(A^t)}.    (N0)

Thus a neutral DOUBLE has BOTH J1,J2 absent. Its old missing labels
are replaced by new missing labels; there need be no A-negative death.

    J absent iff descendants_A^t(LCA_A^t(J))
                 contains an S key outside J.

Two neutral steps need not have distinct blockers, and a persistent

## 3. N1 — at most two ancestor gains during one complete ordinary splay

### Exact statement

Fix two distinct physical keys x,y in ANY actual BST T. During the
complete ordinary bottom-up splay of y, there are at most TWO real
rotations at which x gains a proper ancestor. If y was already an
ancestor of x before the access, there are NONE. Otherwise the gained
ancestors are y, gained once, and possibly one intermediate parent p,
gained at the FIRST rotation of a same-side double. No other gains
occur. In particular

    number of gain rotations for x during splay(y) <= 2,
    depth_after(x) <= depth_before(x)+2.             (N1)

Both statements concern actual FULL-tree ancestry, not labels in a
contracted carrier. Private hanging populations do not affect the proof.

### Proof from the literal rotations

For a right rotation g(p(L,C),R)->p(L,g(C,R)), x gains the
promoted key p precisely when x is g or lies in R. It loses g
precisely when x is p or lies in L. In C it retains both ancestors;
outside the frame it has no ancestry change. Reflection gives the
left-rotation rule. Thus one rotation gains at most one ancestor.

If y is already an ancestor of x, every intermediate parent promoted
in the first rotation of a same-side double is ALREADY an ancestor
of x, and y is already an ancestor too. These are the only promoted
vertices. No rotation can introduce a new ancestor of x. The target
y never gets demoted, so it stays an ancestor throughout the rest
of the access.

Otherwise consider the FIRST StepEv whose whole rotating frame
contains x as well as y. Earlier frames have no effect on ancestors
of x: x is outside them or above them. This first frame exists
because y finishes as the global root. At the end of this StepEv,
y is the root of the frame and a proper ancestor of x. There are
at most two rotations in that event. A zig/zig-zag promotes only
y, so it creates at most one gain; a same-side double promotes
its intermediate parent p and then y, so it creates at most two.
For all subsequent StepEv events use the preceding already-ancestor
argument: no further gains of x are possible.

If two gains occur, the first is p at a same-side first rotation
and the second is y. Every gain is a different newly acquired
ancestor. Depth changes equal gains minus losses, proving the
second bound. QED.

This is the new complete-access bound needed below. It is not the
old single-rotation bound, which by itself permits arbitrarily
many gains in a long splay. It uses the ordinary StepEv grouping.

## 4. N2--N3 — source-reset regeneration witnesses and fixed-key multiplicity

### 4.1 Construction retaining original IDs

Let F' be ANY subset of the original residual doubles, or more
generally any selected actual neutral doubles. For each port
p=(t,k,b), let x=x_t and a_p be the actual demoted pivot. Locate
the previous original KEEP of x in cyclic source order, including
root-repeat KEEPs. Immediately after that operation B has root x,
so a_p is NOT an ancestor of x. Immediately before p it IS one.

Since A-only operations leave B unchanged, there is a last actual
B-rotation gain of a_p as an ancestor of x in this intervening
cyclic interval. Define

    gamma(p)=that last gain occurrence h=(u,k_h,b_h). (N2)

It promotes precisely a_p. Its full-B access target is different
from x; B's own splay of x only loses ancestors of x. From h to
p the physical relation a_p ancestor-of x never disappears.

Every occurrence is one actual original B fine step. The cyclic
interval may wrap the displayed cut ONCE; gamma does not select
another copy's donor or change the legal Step 1 cut. Root repeats,
zero-A operations and all helpers remain in the original interval.

### 4.2 Exact nonreuse rule

The map

    p -> (gamma(p), x_t)                            (REGEN-TAG)

is globally injective on selected ports.

PROOF. A fixed h promotes just one physical ancestor a. For a fixed
x, its relation a ancestor-of x is killed by the FIRST subsequent
KEEP of x. It cannot be consumed at a second x access without a
different gain. Within that first x access the pivot a is demoted
only once. Thus h,x determines at most one original port. QED.

This proof permits B-only neutral transfer cycles: a label cycle
does not prevent reset of physical ancestry at the actual x KEEP.
It never assumes that a missing-label strand reaches an A-negative
rotation or any unused release.

For a fixed intervening B access u and a fixed future target x,
at most TWO selected ports have their gamma inside u, by N1 and
REGEN-TAG. If the demoted ancestor is not a genuine KEEP key,
at most ONE has gamma inside u: the accessed-key gain cannot be
that ancestor; only N1's possible intermediate-parent gain remains.
That anonymous gain is necessarily the first rotation of an actual
same-side B double. This is source evidence for anonymous B
regeneration even if that key stays an unaccessed A leaf.

### 4.3 Quantitative fixed-target theorem

Let K_{!=x} be the number of NONTRIVIAL original B accesses with
target other than x. Root repeats count zero here but are not
removed from the source. Let F'_x be the selected doubles at x.
Then

    2|F'_x| = sum_u #{p at x: gamma(p) lies in u}
             <= 2 K_{!=x},
    |F'_x| <= K_{!=x}.                              (N3a)

The SAME construction can be made for ALL real rotations at x,
not only neutral ports. Every such rotation demotes a different
proper ancestor. If Q_x is their total double count and zeta_x
their total terminal-zig count, then

    2Q_x+zeta_x <= 2 K_{!=x}.                       (N3b)

Locally, for each particular access t of x, 2q_t+zeta_t requires
that many distinct gain rotations since the previous KEEP x;
it requires at least ceil((2q_t+zeta_t)/2) intervening nontrivial
B accesses. Repeating the costly configuration at x requires a
NEW set of regeneration occurrences after its intervening reset.

These are actual recurrence/work restrictions. A numerical bound
on one access alone may be weaker than the inherited whole-core
bound. The additional content is the temporal reset interval,
physical last-gain witnesses, summation across ALL accesses of
the same key, and the distinct anonymous-gain classification.

### 4.4 Associated actual A work, without claiming it free

For each nontrivial B access u, take the original operation segment
AFTER the immediately preceding original KEEP, THROUGH KEEP u.
It begins with A rooted at B's old root g and ends with A rooted
at y_u!=g. Thus it contains at least one actual A StepEv. This
is true if KEEP u itself costs zero because an earlier DELETE
did the work. Every helper is retained. These source segments
are disjoint for different u; they need not be closed or solvent.

Let A_other(x) be the total actual A events in these segments for
nontrivial B accesses with target !=x. Then

    K_{!=x} <= A_other(x),
    |F'_x| <= A_other(x).                            (N3c)

Per fixed x, at most two ports name a given u, while that segment
contains a real actual A event. N3c is consequently a quantitative
bound by DISTINCT source work, including the anonymous-ancestor
case. It does NOT say those event bits are in R1. g1 may own them.

For different x the same u, and its SAME physical A event, can
be used by this argument. Summing N3 over all x without its
intertarget multiplicities would be invalid.

## 5. N4 — an individual regeneration can be shared only by a monotone cohort

### 5.1 Source-defined cohort and strict order rule

Fix one actual regeneration rotation h and its promoted ancestor a.
Let P_h={p:gamma(p)=h}. In a right rotation at old pivot g>a, the
keys gaining a are precisely {g} union keys(R), where R is g's
old right subtree. Every selected target in P_h is therefore >a.
For a left rotation all selected targets are <a.

Read P_h in cyclic time after h. There is at most one port at
any target x, by REGEN-TAG. Moreover:

    right-rotation gain: x_1>x_2>...>x_m>a;
    left-rotation gain:  x_1<x_2<...<x_m<a.           (N4a)

PROOF. Suppose a<x_i. After B splays x_i to the root, EVERY
remaining descendant of a is <x_i. A later target x_j with the
SAME last gain h must still be a descendant of a then: its
relation has not been killed and regenerated. Hence x_j<x_i.
All targets are >a by the literal gain set. Reflection proves
the other order. Distinctness follows as well. QED.

Thus m=|P_h| is at most the number of genuine keys in the
actual one-sided gain set. No constant independent of support
is inferred. Mere reuse of one discrepancy is not free: its
served targets must be different and form this strict record
sequence. A source can regenerate more ancestry, but such new
claims have a different gamma occurrence.

### 5.2 A quantitative work consequence after the regeneration

Start just after the COMPLETE B access containing h, whose target
is y. Every served x_i differs from y. Consider the actual source
intervals ending at the successive served KEEPs:

    end(KEEP y) -> end(KEEP x_1),
    end(KEEP x_1) -> end(KEEP x_2), ... .

At the two ends of each interval A has different roots. Therefore
each contains at least one actual A StepEv. The intervals have
disjoint original operation/event inventories. Consequently

    A(actual interval after access(h) through last served KEEP)
       >= |P_h|.                                   (N4b)

No rootward sign is assumed, and no future-depth claim is used.
The count is of ACTUAL events, not two rotations of one event
counted twice. Intervening KEEPs and all DELETEs are included.

All served ports occur before the same gain could be encountered
again in cyclic order; a live relation cannot pass through its
own next 0->1 gain. The chosen intervals fit in one traversal
of the original period. This is a legal same-period work bound.

Different regeneration cohorts can share those work intervals and
bits. N4b must not be summed over h as if the intervals were
disjoint. Sections 6--8 analyze this actual sharing rather than
using an independent-repayment premise.

## 7. N5 — first-hit consumption of a shared canonical entry chain

### 7.1 Different recurrence argument

The preceding regeneration-own premise fails. Use a DIFFERENT
recurrence restriction: a proper B subtree is rigid until the
first subsequent B target inside it. Couple this fact to the
inherited canonical A-entry chains, not to a presumed donor at
the regenerator.

Fix a canonical A witness beta. All its tagged labels are actual
proper B labels present just after beta. For root-entry beta they
form the inherited strictly nested CHAIN, all containing its new
A root v. A nonroot witness has at most one tag and is immediate.

### 7.2 First-hit and temporal order theorem

For every tagged label I, its port occurs in the FIRST original
KEEP access with target inside I after beta. Call it tau(I).
In particular, if I is strictly contained in J, then

    tau(J) <= tau(I).                               (N5a)

If the inequality is strict, the earlier target belongs to J minus
I. Distinct consumer accesses on one beta chain have distinct
target keys. All tags of a fixed key x at beta are consumed in
ONE x access; a repeated access of x cannot reuse beta.

PROOF. In full B, S is ancestor-closed, so each nonempty proper
label has a unique S-rooted actual subtree. Every B target lies
in S. A B target outside I cannot enter the physical subtree
labelled I; rotations may reattach it as a hanger but leave its
entire structure and label unchanged. A operations do not change
B. Conversely, on the first B access inside I, its whole proper
path label I is removed by the ordinary path rewrite.

The canonical fixed-label run stays missing through beta and
ends at its port. It cannot have lost its B label earlier and
then silently reused the same run. Thus that port's access is
precisely the first hit. Hitting I also hits J if I subset J,
proving N5a. An earlier access at a repeated target would already
have removed every still-live label containing that target.
This proves the distinct-key and nonreuse assertions. QED.

The order statement is about DIFFERENT ACCESSES. Within a single
access the physical SS/LR rotation order remains exactly the four
frames of section2; no fictitious outer-to-inner rotation order
is substituted for them.

### 7.3 Quantitative chain consequence, with all sharing retained

If m_beta different KEEP accesses consume tags of beta, they peel
outer-to-inner label shells and visit m_beta distinct genuine keys.
Hence m_beta<=|M|. Between successive such accesses, A starts and
ends at different roots, so there are at least m_beta-1 DISTINCT
actual A StepEv events in those disjoint original intervals.

This forbids indefinite temporal reuse of the same root entry for
repeated accesses to one key. It does not bound how many nested
labels are consumed WITHIN one costly access: m_beta can equal1
while its port load is large. That within-access load must have
the N2 regeneration history; it is not made free by N5.

No new state quotient, period reduction, donor system or potential
is used. These are restrictions on the actual source occurrence
order of the already-established canonical tags.

## 8. Attempt the universal compensation bridge on source-simple periods

### 8.1 First aggregation: what the proved regeneration counts imply

Take F'=F and write ell_u(x) for the number of residual ports at

    2|F|=sum_u sum_x ell_u(x),
