## 2. Exact reduction in the no-release class

from the +1,-1,0 changes of Psi at ACTUAL A single rotations, and V_A
from the virtual second units of terminal A zigs. These are disjoint
accounting objects, so

    2A = P+N+T_A+V_A.

The original closure law is

    P=N+B1+2B2+Z1.

Since B2 and Z1 are nonnegative event counts, total B2=Z1=0 means
their per-access values are all zero. Thus

    P=N+B1,       Q_B=B0+B1,

This derivation is exact. It does NOT prove that the right side is

The final B-rotation theorem gives two further source restrictions:

* Every genuine nonrepeat B access has even positive depth. An odd

* Its last double is B1: the final rotation decreases Psi, B2 is
  forbidden, and B0 is neutral. Therefore every neutral double is
  strictly internal.

## 3. Two different credits per negative A rotation

For every actual negative A rotation at fine occurrence delta, let
u_delta be its own physical rotation bit and let lambda(delta) be the
positive A bit matched to its actual decrease slot by the EXISTING
cyclic level bijection. The free inventory is exactly the disjoint union

    U_N = {u_delta : delta is an A-negative rotation},
    H_N = {lambda(delta) : delta is an A-negative rotation},
    U_T = {actual zero-Psi A rotation bits},
    U_V = {virtual second A-zig units}.

Then

    R1 = C_A minus image(g1) = U_N disjoint-union H_N
                              disjoint-union U_T disjoint-union U_V,
    |R1| = N+N+T_A+V_A.                         (2)

PROOF. The positive bits are partitioned by lambda among B1 payment
slots and A-negative release slots, since there are no B releases.
The former are exactly image(g1); the latter are H_N. Every other unit
is its own negative bit, a zero-rotation bit, or a virtual zig unit.
Those categories are pairwise disjoint. All H_N bits are positive
rotation bits, so none is in the other three categories. Lambda is
injective, so different negative slots have different H_N heads. QED.

In particular the coefficient2 on N does NOT mean "use both bits of
the negative rotation's StepEv." One credit is its own negative bit;
the other is a DIFFERENT positive bit, potentially at a different
original access. The event's other bit has its own classification.

Both endpoints of a cyclic pairing remain actual IDs on W. A decrease
before its donor in the displayed linear cut uses the donor's later
ACTUAL occurrence on this same period. It does not borrow another copy.
Freedom in (2) is freedom relative to raw g1. Independent h0/fE/Gamma
reservations are not retired or declared free by this identity.

## 4. New source-uniform lemma: zero A work fixes the missing-label set

THEOREM. Hold the actual full B tree fixed during any original A single
rotation. With the original ancestor-closed B carrier S, put

    M(A)=J_S(B) minus J_S(A).

That A rotation cannot both remove a member from M and add a different
member to M. Consequently

    DeltaPsi=0  =>  M_after=M_before,
    DeltaPsi=+1 =>  one pure missing-label birth,
    DeltaPsi=-1 =>  one pure missing-label death.         (3)

This is uniform over support, arbitrary A private ancestors and hangers,
and arbitrary B S-free hangers. It does not require B2=Z1=0. It uses
the existing Psi and original trees, not a new potential or quotient.

### Proof, including anonymous operands and duplicates

Consider a right rotation in an arbitrary unchanged A context:

    p(x(L,C),R) -> x(L,p(C,R)).

Let

    X=(S intersect keys(L)) union ({x} if x in S),
    Y=S intersect keys(C),
    Z=({p} if p in S) union (S intersect keys(R)).

These are ordered disjoint parts. The only candidate lost and gained
proper A labels are respectively

    K=X union Y,       J=Y union Z.

Their union W=X union Y union Z is the S-label of the whole A frame
and is an interval of the ordered set S. Its label persists at the new
root; unchanged subtrees retain every other label. To BOTH add and
remove a missing label requires all of the following:

1. K actually disappears from the A SET and belongs to J_S(B).
2. J actually appears in the A SET and belongs to J_S(B).

If X is empty, K=Y. When Y is nonempty, the unchanged actual subtree C
still supplies Y after the rotation, so K cannot disappear. When Y is
empty, K is not a nonempty B label at all. Thus simultaneous change
requires X nonempty.

If Z is empty, J=Y. For nonempty Y the unchanged subtree C supplied Y
already BEFORE the rotation, so J cannot be a newly appearing A label;
the empty case again cannot change M. Thus Z is also nonempty.

If Y is nonempty, K and J overlap but neither contains the other,
because X,Z are nonempty. Two B subtree labels cannot do that: actual
subtrees are nested or disjoint. They therefore cannot both belong to
J_S(B).

If Y is empty, K=X and J=Z are disjoint, adjacent nonempty intervals
whose union is W. Let a=max(K), b=min(J). No S key lies strictly between
a and b, since W is an interval and is their union. If K,J were both
B subtree labels, the B LCA of a,b would be an S vertex: S is ancestor-
closed. A BST LCA key lies between a and b, so that LCA must be a or b.
That would make one of a,b an ancestor of the other, impossible when
they belong to two disjoint B subtrees. Again K,J cannot both occur.

All cases contradict simultaneous addition and removal. Reflection gives
the left-rotation case by reversing order and swapping child pointers.
SET duplications caused by private unary nodes were handled explicitly
when X or Z is empty; extra duplicates can only prevent a putative
disappearance/appearance, not invalidate the exclusion. The known
one-label bounds now give all three conclusions in (3). QED.

### Consequences for source compensation

The old lifetime normal form permissively allowed an A zero-change
transfer. Lemma (3) proves that such a transfer does not occur in an
actual source at the ORIGINAL carrier S. This strengthens that forward
description; it does not invalidate its path/cycle theorem or old maps.
Actual transfers of missing labels occur only in B rotations.

Thus T_A is NOT work that transfers missing-label strands. All its
bits remain real and raw-free, but they are invisible to the strand
graph's births, deaths and transfers. Paying a neutral B double from
T_A requires another actual source relationship; counting a fictitious
A transfer cannot provide it.

Combining (3) with the Step 2 canonical rule gives an exact dichotomy:

* An A-addition canonical witness is always a POSITIVE rotation. In
  the present no-release class its bit is either a fixed B1 payment or
  one of the A-negative release heads H_N.
* A zero or negative canonical witness must instead be a ROOT ENTRY.
  It occurs at a global root change, within the last A StepEv.
* An inner zero rotation and a virtual zig unit can never themselves
  be canonical ROTATION witnesses. They may still be usable through
  the other bit of a witnessed event, an OWN edge, or a certified
  normalization path. The graph that includes BOTH event bits must not
  be confused with the rotation-tag map.

At root entry, the same original B-label chain may attach many ports to
one actual bit. Neither (3) nor B2=Z1=0 proves that such a chain has
length at most two or that its two event bits are unoccupied. Consequently
the new lemma does not prove |F|<=|R1|. It identifies exactly why the
missing relation cannot be only missing-strand conservation.
