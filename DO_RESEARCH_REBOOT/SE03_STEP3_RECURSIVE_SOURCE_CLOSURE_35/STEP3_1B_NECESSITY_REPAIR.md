## 2. Which actual B rotation can destroy ancestry, and what it pays

Consider any actual right rotation in full B:

    p(v(L,C),R) -> v(L,p(C,R)).                       (1)

It destroys precisely the proper-ancestor relations (p,b) for physical
b in keys(L) union {v}. It gains precisely (v,b) for b in {p} union
keys(R). For b in C the ancestor SET is unchanged. Reflection gives
the left rotation. This is the literal pointer identity; it does not
require prepared return geometry.

Consequently the first loss of any persistent B ancestry relation must
be one of these actual rotations. A-only helpers cannot change B
ancestry. One B rotation can destroy many such relations at once,
and many demands can also refer to the same lost relation. Counting
one disruption per relation or per claim would duplicate the event.

During the actual B splay of x, the removed proper S-label I contains
x and is missing from A, whose root is already x. The new proper
label J excludes x. Both rotation operands belong to the original
carrier. The exact inherited identity is

    DeltaPsi_B = -1 + 1_{J not in J_S(A)}.            (2)

Therefore the disruption has two alternatives:

* J is present in A: a B decrease slot is generated.
* J is absent in A: I is replaced by J in M, with DeltaPsi=0.
  This is a neutral transfer, with NO decrease slot and NO new A bit.

Even the first alternative is not automatically unused capacity.
Its actual positive head lambda(slot) is governed by Step 1:

| Slot type | Exact physical status |
|---|---|
| First decrease of a nonneutral B double | Its head is that double's fixed g1 payment. |
| Second B2 decrease or terminal B-zig release, selected by Step 1 | Its head pays an existing neutral demand. |
| Such a release left unselected | Its one actual head is in R1. |

Thus destroying B ancestry can make a demand, transfer a missing label,
or generate a payment already committed to another demand. It is not
an independently proved credit-creation operation.
