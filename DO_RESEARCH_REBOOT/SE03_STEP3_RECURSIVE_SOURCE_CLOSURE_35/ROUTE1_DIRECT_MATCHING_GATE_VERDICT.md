## 4. Exact missing bridge, without relabeling the main theorem

At the EXISTING GRAPH algorithm's interface, the unproved requirement is
source-forced completion/augmentation capacity. A statement saying that
every unfinished alternating search reaches a free unit, or that every
candidate neighborhood satisfies Hall's inequality, is a restatement of
full matchability for that graph. It is NOT offered here as a smaller
new lemma, and it is not assumed. Dropping the graph and assuming
|R_1|>=|F(W)| is likewise just the missing scalar funding inequality.

The smallest independently checkable LOCAL bridge already available in
the inspected complete augmentation machinery is the original source-
solvent block certificate, at its unchanged conditions:

    B is a specified consecutive union of original natural macros;
    an independent source argument establishes 2H_B >= Q_B;
    the admitted partial comparison extends h0 and is B-closed;
    original static block eligibility/reservations hold;
    actual empty-bank resets hold if splicing an existing trace.

This local certificate really suffices for the EXISTING ordinal completion
theorem; its source proof may not assume the desired global CD inequality.
Steps 1–2 establish none of the missing solvency/closure/reset properties
on arbitrary blocks. They also do not force a disjoint complete cover by
such certified blocks. Taking B=W and assuming 2A>=Q_B would not supply a
bridge. A valid cover constructed from h0 could instead supply full h,
then raw normalization could preserve g_1; no cover or h is built here.

For the purely direct graph approach, the first uncovered source structure
is a nested top-event chain with occupied witnesses and interacting
blocking owners, including possible seams and shared components. Existing
relay hypotheses are not established for those owners. No inspected result
reduces all such structures to the local certificates above or proves a
smaller sufficient capacity bound for them. This report therefore cannot
honestly identify a single small universal lemma whose proof is known to
close the route; claiming otherwise would hide the original global burden.
