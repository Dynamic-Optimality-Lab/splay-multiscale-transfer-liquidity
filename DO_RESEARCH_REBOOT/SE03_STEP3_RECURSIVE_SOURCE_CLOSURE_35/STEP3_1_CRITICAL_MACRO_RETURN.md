# Step 3.1 — critical-macro return lemma

**B — PARTIAL.** A quantitative original-source return theorem is
proved for discrepant ancestors actually visited by A outside the
critical macro. It always covers the genuine KEEP part of the
discrepancy. A sharing-aware ledger avoids counting a separate return
for every critical macro. In the existing fully marked PC35 family,
the actual A-only return segment has at least m A StepEv events.

The stronger claim that EVERY discrepant B ancestor must individually
return through A ancestry is refuted by the saved critical CCR source:
some discrepant ancestors remain unaccessed A leaves throughout the
period. No general quantitative A bound charging that anonymous part
by another mechanism is proved here. No Step 3.2 work is attempted.

## 2. Literal ancestry-change lemma

Consider ONE actual right rotation in an arbitrary physical context:

    p(v(L,C),R) -> v(L,p(C,R)).

For a fixed physical target x, the proper-ancestor SET changes as follows:

| Position of x | Ancestor-set change |
|---|---|
| x=v or x is in L | Lose the old pivot p, and no other ancestor. |
| x=p or x is in R | Gain the promoted child v, and no other ancestor. |
| x is in C | No set change: p,v remain ancestors, though their order swaps. |
| x is outside the rotated subtree | No change. |

This follows directly from the displayed pointers. Reflection gives
the left rotation: swap L/R and reverse the key order. No population
bound or marked-only contraction is used.

Therefore one actual rotation changes at most ONE proper ancestor of
a FIXED target, and its depth by at most1. For any actual A interval I,

    R_A(I) >= |Anc_{A_start}(x) symmetric-difference
               Anc_{A_end}(x)|.                         (1)

More strongly, the number of distinct selected ancestry gain/loss
occurrences for that fixed x is at most R_A(I). Gains of ancestor u
have promoted child u; losses of ancestor u have demoted pivot u.
All such witnesses retain their original rotation bit(t,j,b).

For DIFFERENT targets the same rotation can witness many changes.
For the displayed right rotation, its loss targets are keys(L) union
{v}, and its gain targets are {p} union keys(R), two disjoint ordered
physical-key intervals. It is still ONE rotation bit. This is the
exact source sharing rule, not permission to spend that bit per target.

## 3. Reconstructing and sharpening the inherited discrepancy bound

Put a=depth_{A^-}(x), b=depth_{B^-}(x). The final B splay has exactly

    b=2Q_e+zeta_e

actual rotations. The whole A macro, including every helper, ends with
x at its root. By section2, reducing its depth from a to0 needs at
least a actual rotations, even when other keys are accessed first.
Thus a<=R_A(e)=2H_e-V_e. Consequently

    |D_e| >= b-a
           >= 2(Q_e-H_e)+zeta_e+V_e.                  (2)

The given lower bound |D_e|>=2(Q_e-H_e) follows. This is a bound at
the original START, not a substitution of the final A-access depth
for the complete macro cost. It remains valid if the final KEEP has
A cost0 after an earlier DELETE of x.

Both x and g are genuine keys. The shared root g belongs to both proper
ancestor sets and hence not D_e; x is not its own proper ancestor.

## 4. Externally visited ancestor return theorem

Let bar(e) be the original cyclic complement: start just AFTER e and
follow the original source until just BEFORE e. Every outside operation
appears once with its original ID, even if this interval wraps the
displayed cut. Define

    E_e={u in D_e : some original A access to u occurs in bar(e)},
    kappa_e=|E_e|.

The access may be KEEP or DELETE and may have zero A cost because an
earlier helper has already brought u to the root. No access is invented.

### Theorem

For every actual critical macro in the original source domain, there
are at least TWO DISTINCT actual A ancestry-change rotations in bar(e)
for each u in E_e. For this fixed x, all selected rotations over
different u are distinct. Hence

    R_A(bar(e)) = 2(A(W)-H_e)-(V_A(W)-V_e)
                  >= 2*kappa_e,                       (3)

and in event units

    A(W)-H_e >= kappa_e + ceil((V_A(W)-V_e)/2).        (4)

This is a return-work bound, not an allocation of unused credits.

### Proof

At the start of bar(e), A has root x, so u is NOT an ancestor of x.
At its end, A is exactly A^- by original A closure, so u is again
NOT an ancestor of x, by the definition of D_e.

Choose an actual outside access to u. Immediately after its A splay,
A has root u, making u a proper ancestor of the distinct target x.
Thus the actual indicator of "u is an A ancestor of x" changes

    0 -> 1 -> 0

on that same outside interval. Choose the last gain before that visited
root snapshot and the first loss after it. They exist and are distinct
actual A rotations in bar(e). A zero-cost access does not fabricate
a rotation: its gain lies at a preceding actual source operation.

By section2, for the fixed target x one rotation can change only one
ancestor membership. So rotations selected for different u cannot
coincide. There are at least2*kappa_e distinct actual rotations; (3)
and (4) follow from the exact real-rotation/event identity. QED.

All bits named by this proof are original real rotation bits. An A
double can contribute at most its two actual rotations, and a terminal
zig only its one rotation. Its virtual unit is not counted as a
return witness. No reaching prefix or neighboring period supplies work.

### Guaranteed genuine part and quantitative corollaries

Every u in D_e intersect M belongs to E_e. Indeed u differs from both
g and x. A KEEP u inside the macro would change B's root g and would
be a genuine macro endpoint before x, contrary to e's definition.
Therefore its actual genuine KEEP lies outside e. DELETE u inside e
does not invalidate this separate outside KEEP.

Since at most c-r B ancestors are nongenuine,

    kappa_e >= |D_e intersect M|
      >= max(0, 2(Q_e-H_e)+zeta_e+V_e-(c-r)).          (5)

This holds for arbitrary genuine support, arbitrary helpers and private
A geometry. It can be zero for the genuine part of a large anonymous
discrepancy; no nonzero bound is asserted where (5) does not give one.

On the declared class D_e subset E_e, EVERY discrepant ancestor has
an actual outside visit, and the full lower bound yields

    R_A(bar(e)) >= 2|D_e|
                 >=4(Q_e-H_e)+2zeta_e+2V_e.          (6)

In particular (6) holds when the original B carrier is fully genuine
(S=M), with no restriction on A's private nodes or helper population.

There is also a useful maximum-depth consequence. If an actual macro
has Q_e=r-1 in the closed r-key source, the core bound c<=2r-1 forces
c=2r-1 and zeta_e=0. Then

    kappa_e >= Q_e-2H_e+V_e,
    R_A(bar(e)) >=2(Q_e-2H_e+V_e).                   (7)

The right side is positive for a critical macro. This uses only the
actual maximum-depth condition and the genuine subset. It does not
assume that each anonymous B ancestor changes its A ancestry.

The counts in (3)-(7) must not be added independently for many macros.
The same original work can belong to several outside intervals. The
sharing-safe statement is the next section.

## 5. Shared work: a fixed-target return ledger

Fix ONE genuine target x and any chosen finite collection of its critical
macros. For each other genuine key u, list its actual KEEP occurrences
in cyclic source order, including root repeats. Consecutive KEEP u
occurrences delimit the original cyclic u-gaps. At each endpoint the
actual A root is u, so u is an ancestor of x.

Call a u-gap ACTIVE if it contains at least one selected macro e of
target x with u in D_e. Such a macro contains no KEEP u, so lies within
one such gap. Its A start and end both have u NOT an ancestor of x.

For every active gap choose:

* its first actual loss of u's ancestry before the first such macro;
* its last actual gain after the last such macro and by the next KEEP u.

The 1->0->1 endpoint logic proves these witnesses exist. They lie
outside all the u-discrepant macros assigned to THAT gap, though they
may lie inside another macro associated with a DIFFERENT u. All original
helpers remain; no gap is assumed to be a closed or independently
funded source block.

Let G_x be the number of DISTINCT active pairs(u,u-gap). Then

    2G_x <= R_A(W)=2A(W)-V_A(W).                      (8)

Proof of noncollision: for a fixed u, different u-gaps have disjoint
actual ancestry-change occurrences. For different u but this SAME x,
one rotation cannot be both selected changes by section2. Thus the
2G_x chosen rotations are distinct. QED.

Several critical macros in the same active u-gap contribute ONE witness
pair, not one pair per macro. In general G_x is NOT sum_e |D_e intersect M|.
For different x, witness bits may coincide exactly as the two target
intervals in section2 describe. It would be invalid to sum (8) over
targets while treating those bits as independent repayments.

This is a source-uniform sharing rule for actual return WORK. It is
not a new potential and does not promise free credits or an intertarget
capacity bound. Pure cardinality (4) may be weaker than other known
whole-period visit counts; the additional information is the forced
gain/loss identities, their positions, and their actual sharing classes.

### Exact quantitative return theorem for Gamma

For EVERY actual ordinary-splay realization of that existing return,

    R_A(Gamma)>=n-1=2m,
    2H_Gamma-V_Gamma>=2m,
    H_Gamma>=m+ceil(V_Gamma/2)>=m.                   (9)

PROOF. During the descending scan, the literal A state after KEEP k is

    k(1(empty,L_[2,k-1]), R_[k+1,n]),  2<=k<=n.

The induction is an actual LR splay of k-1 from depth2; all higher
keys form the right vine and remain rigid. In particular

    A_after_KEEP2=2(1,R_[3,n]).

Before Gamma, the proper A ancestors of target n are {2,...,n-1}.
After Gamma they are {1}. The symmetric difference has size n-1.
Section2 therefore forces at least n-1 actual rotations in Gamma;
the exact event/zig identity gives (9). QED.

This phase contains no genuine B demand, but its original A work is
charged and cannot be erased. The proof is stronger than simply
assuming a future target becomes deep: it uses the fixed ACTUAL
return endpoints of this declared family. It holds for any choice
of its inherited ordinary-splay Gamma transformation, not just a
finite stored example. It does not assert that an arbitrary dirty
source contains such a scan or such a Gamma segment.

The general return theorem is valid with arbitrary A private ancestors;

### Exact counterexample to the stronger per-ancestor mechanism

    1(empty,7(3(2,5(4,6)),11(9(8,10),13(12,empty)))).

A bare number here denotes that physical leaf. Every even key

An unaccessed leaf cannot lie on a search path to any other key.

## 8. Exact scope and first remaining implication

The return constraint is fully proved on the externally visited part
E_e. In particular it gives (6) for the declared class D_e subset E_e,
including fully genuine B carriers. For arbitrary sources, the genuine
subset always gives the quantitative lower bound (5), and actual
maximum-depth critical macros additionally give (7).

What remains is a different source-geometric implication for unvisited
anonymous B ancestors. B closure forces their B ancestry to return;
it does not force their SAME A ancestry to appear, as CCR proves.
Converting that B return into other quantitative A work needs an
independently justified association. No such association is assumed
or constructed in this report.

Even for visited ancestors, source witnesses for different targets can
share a rotation's ordered target intervals. The proved per-target
ledger prevents independent per-macro counting, but it does not give
a globally injective credit allocation. Those observations state the
interface limits; they are not an execution of Step 3.2.

The full-state source-simple reduction is preserved. An ancestry-return
gap need not have equal full A/B endpoints and is not thereby a closed
proof child or an independently funded block. The lemma holds directly
on source-simple histories as well; it neither forces another state
repetition nor assumes a shorter solvent source.
