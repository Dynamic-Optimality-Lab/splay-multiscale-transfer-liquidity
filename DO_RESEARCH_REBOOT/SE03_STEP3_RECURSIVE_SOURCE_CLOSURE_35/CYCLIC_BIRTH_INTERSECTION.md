# BIRTH35 — at most two shared first-retirement tickets, PROVED

Target: compress the source interactions of ANY two genuine macros.
Conditions: inherited closed maximal full core/path-to-star domain.
Inheritance: after a genuine access all its old marked ancestors become
children of the target, and only the accessed marked key can grow its
marked descendants. All A/private populations remain arbitrary.
Dual: a pair with more than one unrefreshed common ancestor on one cyclic
arc, or more than two shared first-rotation ticket units.
Bridge: a necessary source restriction on potential countercuts, not a
proof of their capacity. No support/word-length enumeration is used.

Let m,n be distinct genuine macro occurrences. Define F as the keys
which are old marked B ancestors of BOTH targets and are NOT genuinely
accessed on the open cyclic arc from m through n. Then |F|<=1.

Proof. Immediately after m, all keys in F are distinct marked children
of its target. Their marked descendant subtrees are disjoint. Until n,
none of those keys is accessed, so its descendant set only shrinks.
If two remained ancestors of n's target, that target would belong to
two initially disjoint sets. Impossible. QED. Applying this argument to
the other arc gives at most one reverse-unrefreshed common ancestor.
The two sets are disjoint: every genuine marked key is genuinely accessed
somewhere on the closed period, and neither endpoint target can be its
own strict ancestor.

For each demand use its old B ancestor g and its first A rotation after
the last genuine g KEEP losing g's ancestry of the demand target.
AR1/OR34 give a unique pivot g and a real physical rotation bit, and
different g within one macro give distinct tickets. If two macros share
a ticket, their birth-to-demand intervals overlap at that operation.
Their most recent g births must be the same occurrence (with the actual
cyclic alignment), so one of the two intervening arcs has no g KEEP.
Thus g belongs to one of the two sets above. There are at most two
shared tickets between the two individual first-ticket images. QED.

The same g may retire many times after A-only regrowth. This does NOT
bound the intersection of the FULL operation pools by2, nor the size
of a many-macro collision group. The theorem concerns shared FIRST
rotation tickets. proof171 checks this distinction on accepted states;
the universal claim follows from the source argument, not those checks.
