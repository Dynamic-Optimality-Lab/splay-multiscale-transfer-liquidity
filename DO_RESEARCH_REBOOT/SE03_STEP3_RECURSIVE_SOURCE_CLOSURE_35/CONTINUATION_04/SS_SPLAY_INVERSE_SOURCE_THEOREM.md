## 1. Literal frame conjugation

For an RR outer frame the source is

    G(A,U(B,L(C,D))),       G<U<all keys in L's subtree.

Perform any legal inner SS access y in the marked L subtree. At the
end of its inner work write the subtree as y(C',D'). The remaining
outer RR gives

    y(U(G(A,B),C'),D').

Now genuinely access G. Its source path is y,U,G, an LL double. It
restores EXACTLY

    G(A,U(B,y(C',D'))).

All four outside subtrees are arbitrary ordered terms; no private node
or physical operation was deleted. Thus the full word y,G implements
one inner access while restoring the outer frame. The LL version is
the literal reflection. The inner subtree's key SET stays in its same
ordered interval, so this outer SS condition remains true for every
letter of any inner word, not merely its first letter.

## 2. Inverse of one original full access

Define a finite original marked-key word by

    Inv([])=[],
    Inv([G0]+tail)=[G0]+concat([y,G0] for y in Inv(tail)).

For q=1 this is [G0]; the opposite SS double reverses the two original
rotations exactly. For general q, the full original splay first does
its q-1 inner doubles and then one outer root exchange. Access G0
undoes that OUTER exchange, leaving the inner transformed subtree in
the original outside frame. By induction Inv(tail) restores the inner
subtree. Lift each of its letters by the conjugation y,G0 from Section1.
The original ENTIRE B tree is restored, including all hanging BSTs.

All inverse letters are original marked ancestors; every full operation
is an ordinary genuine SS splay with even depth. There are no synthetic
transitions, physical deletions, new marked labels, or terminal zigs.
The induction decreases the actual original double count q.

Let L(q) be inverse word length and C(q) its original B-double cost.
The exact recurrences are

    L(q)=1+2L(q-1),
    C(q)=1+C(q-1)+2L(q-1),       L(0)=C(0)=0.

Therefore, for q>=1,

    L(q)=2^q-1,       C(q)=2^(q+1)-q-2.
