## 1. The MD version of reverse normalization

Each actual B double at genuine consumer x has a different marked
grandparent G. Choose its first actual A rotation losing G->x after
the last genuine G. MD35 proves these witnesses exist; for fixed x
their source intervals are disjoint over repeated genuine x accesses.
Group by the identical physical loss bit and order consumers within
the common actual G tenure. Keep the bit for the last member; each
earlier member is a SELF claim in the sense of RLC35. The reverse
root-contact proof applies verbatim. Do not substitute arbitrary FP
tags in the B-subtree arguments that follow.

For a SELF claim d aimed at x, write z(d) for the NEXT consumer in that
same original direct group and G(d) for its B grandparent proxy. Its
actual future contact KEEP z occurs before the next genuine G and has
G as an actual marked B grandparent. In particular z!=G and z!=x.

## 2. The exact marked-fringe bound at one consumer

Let an actual consumer x have q original B doubles. Immediately after
its B splay all q old marked grandparents are nearest marked children
of x. Their FULL rooted physical B subtrees are pairwise disjoint;
this is the original marked-parent projection, not literal-child
identification and not a static-tree claim at later boundaries.

Between x and z(d), G(d) is never genuinely accessed. Its marked
descendant set can only shrink. Since G(d) is a grandparent at z(d),
that key lies in its original subtree IMMEDIATELY AFTER x. It is a
proper marked descendant, different from G(d).

For different SELF demands of this SAME x macro, the original proxies
G are different and their post-x subtrees are disjoint. Hence the
future contact keys z(d) are pairwise distinct and are not x or ANY
of the q old grandparents. Counting the actual distinct marked keys

    q + number_SELF_at_x <= r-1.                    (MFR)

No private-inventory bound or finite support enumeration occurs here.
In particular a q=r-1 macro has NO SELF claims in this normalization;

Abstract overlaps in which two such demands at x have the same next

## 4. Two simultaneous noncollision theorems

**One source consumer x:** all selected contact keys z are distinct by
Section 2. The b(d) have different actual rotation pivots. They are
therefore distinct physical bits, even if their source intervals or
StepEv events overlap. Distinct rotations in one event retain their
different bit labels. Every SELF demand of this macro is paid once
by this explicit raw source rule.

**One original proxy key G:** within any G tenure, actual B demotions
of G have distinct genuine targets by marked descendant shrinkage.
The NEXT group members used as contacts are distinct original demands;
each has at most one predecessor in its own direct group. Therefore
their keys z are distinct in this G tenure. Return bits have different
pivots. Across different G tenures their return intervals lie strictly
between that tenure's boundaries, so physical bit inventories are
disjoint. ALL SELF claims with old proxy G, for arbitrary repetitions
and source length, have one simultaneous injective supplier map.

Consequently any remaining collision b(d)=b(e) must involve BOTH
different original consumer occurrences and different old proxy keys.
The common physical pivot forces the contact KEY z(d)=z(e). Contacts
may be the same genuine occurrence (different B grandparents there)
or different occurrences of that key. Neither case is excluded.
