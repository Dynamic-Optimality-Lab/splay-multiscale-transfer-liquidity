## 1. Exact physical B-edge-run ledger

    R=(e,gamma_R),

    physical edge e;

    complete child subtree and parent context immediately after birth;
    complete child subtree and parent context immediately before death;

### 2.2 A bijective transfer, not newly minted capacity

\tag{2.1}

for each real B rotation. Its changed part has exactly 1+1[c exists]+1[H nonempty] old and new edges. It never sends one old unit to two new edges.

### 2.3 Periodicity, bundles and winding

Choose one actual cut. Name a transported unit by its physical edge at that cut, and propagate it using (2.1). After one period the complete B edge set returns, but the names may be permuted. Let pi be that permutation.

A cycle of pi of length omega contains omega simultaneously live physical edge units at the cut, NOT one credit. Its set of occupied edges is rigorously periodic because pi only permutes names within the cycle. Equivalently, following the run-transfer cycle through causal lifts may wind omega times around the source. That winding does not create omega additional copies of any physical A event.

For such a periodic bundle C define

\[
\phi_C(s)=\#\{\text{its occupied physical B edges absent from }E(A_s)\}.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_C\phi_C(s)=|E(B_s)\setminus E(A_s)|=\Phi_0(s),
\quad \phi_C(\text{end})=\phi_C(\text{start}).
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is an exact rigorously periodic capacity decomposition. It supplies no inequality for the number of neutral marks on a bundle.

All S-to-S edges are ACTUAL physical edges. The role bijection preserves that type; a middle row with S-free child stays an S-free-child boundary. At a cut there are |S|-1 S-to-S edges. Persistent S-free internal edges and all hanger boundaries remain in the full ledger; they are not contracted.

An A rotation can change agreement on at most six currently occupied physical B edges (the union of at most three old and three new A edges). Hence the total bundle-touch multiplicity of one A rotation is at most six. One B rotation changes at most three transported units. These bounded incidence facts do NOT prove that every neutral demand is paid by such a touch.

## 3. What a canonical consumption actually forces

### 3.1 No same-unit duplication inside one B access

Track the transferred unit on R_1(d). After a COMPLETE SS double it is the edge from demoted parent p to old grandparent g, lying below target z. After LR/RL, its external-row transfer in bit 0 followed by its pivot reversal in bit 1 leaves it on z->g, again at/below z.

\[
\boxed{\text{one transported edge unit is a canonical index
at most once in one original B access}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Counting the actual |S|-1 S-to-S strands at a cut gives the nonuniform consequence U_int<=(|S|-1)K_B. This is not an absolute bound: source size remains. It is not a new URCD certificate.

### 3.2 A universal output-run renewal record

### 3.3 A literal failure of current-edge-touch-only renewal

A tempting binary rule would spend a unit at a neutral canonical consumption and replenish it only when an A rotation changes its CURRENT physical B edge, or a decreasing B rotation touches that unit in its changed-edge rows. That rule is false.
