## D. Source-derived birth geometry

The following elementary consequences use full ordinary B operations. They do not assume sparsity, a new potential, or an unused owner.

### D1. Target-created side chains

Freeze one original B access to z. A rotation is a target promotion if the promoted node is the actual target z. All other promotions in ordinary bottom-up splay are intermediate-parent promotions in the FIRST rotation of a same-side double.

When z is promoted, the demoted pivot becomes one child of z. Its new proper S label lies wholly on that side of z. It persists to the end of this access: z is never subsequently demoted in its own splay, and later rotations above z transport that whole subtree.

List all labels created by target promotions on the right side, in creation order. At the next such promotion, the old right child of z becomes the LEFT child of the new demoted pivot. Other-side promotions and intermediate-parent rotations preserve that old subtree. Thus the successive new labels are strictly nested and their roots become adjacent along a left-child chain.

There is an identical reflected left-side chain, with adjacent right-child roots. The actual source labels and physical hangers are retained.

A right target-created label starts at succ_S(z); a left label ends at pred_S(z). This follows because the rotation frame contains z and its demoted S pivot, hence every S key between them. It excludes z and contains the entire within-frame side.

### D2. First-SS inner outputs are disjoint within an access

For a right-right double,

    g(L,p(H,z(C,R))),

the first left rotation creates the demoted g label from L,{g},H. It excludes the WHOLE current z subtree. After the completed double, z is the root of the entire old g frame and contains that first output.

Reflection proves the left-left case. The same exclusion argument works across different same-side orientations in the access. Consequently ALL first-SS intermediate-parent outputs in one B access are pairwise disjoint S intervals.

In particular at most one of them contains any fixed carrier key. This is a birth-output statement, not a newly claimed version of the inherited creator-forest multiplicity theorem.
