# Three distinct feasibility problems

1. Original uniform counting eligibility: every remaining root may use any
   actual free bit in I. Since a is injective, |U\im(a)|-|F\dom(a)|=|U|-|F|.
   Hall's condition then reduces to the same total capacity condition. This
   observation does not prove that condition for original sources and is
   not adopted as the desired source-funding theorem.
2. Licensed matching: source blocks/reservations can restrict usable units.
   Those restrictions must come from the actual source contract, not a
   current partial comparison's image. A free raw bit is not automatically
   a licensed endpoint under a supplied normalized owner map.
3. Storage/transport: a working queue or an endpoint schedule needs its own
   proof. Static Hall expansion does not prove either. Conversely failing
   one chosen storage rule does not refute every legal source construction.

Gamma55 decisively separates1 from the stronger all-event endpoint version
of3. It has explicit complete count matching and owner-preserving extension,
yet EVERY matching has endpoint peak>=27. This follows from original B
event geometry, not from a selected algorithm failing. The original GateU14
working-buffer version of3 remains a different, conditional source obligation.
