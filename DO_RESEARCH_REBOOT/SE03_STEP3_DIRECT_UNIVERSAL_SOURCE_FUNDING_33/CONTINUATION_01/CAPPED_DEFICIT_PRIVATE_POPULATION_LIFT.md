## Lemma 1: active source exposure

For one BST rotation, each fixed key's depth changes by at most1. In the
left-child rotation, the child, parent and three hanging terms change depth
respectively by-1,+1,-1,0,+1; reflection covers the other orientation.
An actual A access of depth d uses ceil(d/2) StepEv events and exactly d
single rotations. Thus P uses at most2H<=2h single rotations.

Let U be the union of every actual A search path in P, together with the
initial root. Initially only that root is exposed. Exposed nodes are an
ancestor-closed prefix in the INITIAL A tree; the unexposed components are
its original rigid hanging terms. A new search enters at most one such
term, exposes the original path to its target, and leaves all remaining
components rigid. This proves initial ancestor closure by induction.
An access of depth d adds at most d new keys, because its current root was
already exposed. Consequently |U|<=1+sum d_i<=1+2H.

If u occurs on the i-th actual path, its current depth is at most d_i.
Earlier rotations changed its initial depth by at most their count. Hence

    initial_depth_A(u)<=sum_(j<i)d_j+d_i<=2H<=2h.

This includes newly exposed anonymous helpers and every rotating operand;
none is erased as an unobserved private node. A zero-event access repeats
the current already exposed root and introduces no new operand.
