## Exact obstruction at a justified factorable seed policy

If the physical joint repair fails in one policy fiber L satisfying F,
choose an inclusion-minimal nonempty X subset R violating QH. Equivalently
`|N_J(X)|<|X|`. Then:

    |N_J(X)| = |X|-1;                  X intersect B is nonempty;
    |N_L(X) \ k(X\B)| = |X intersect B|-1.                 (MC)

Proof. For any x in X, minimality gives at least |X|-1 neighbors for
X\{x}; they are contained in N_J(X). For a singleton the same lower bound
is zero. The strict deficiency therefore equals one. If X contained no
root, its distinct k-neighbors would already supply |X| neighbors, a
contradiction. Subtract |X\B| for the last equation. QED.

All proper subsets meet the Hall bounds, so this is a genuinely JOINT
obstruction, possibly involving already-paid R holes as relay vertices.
The source demands in X each occur once; paid members are not billed twice.

For the stronger independent-comparison E_r2 fiber, use inclusion-minimal
X violating FH instead: `|N_L(X)|=|X|-1`. No owner stay is then available.
It need not meet B, since the parent asks for an independent comparison
even for a hole whose physical demand was already paid.

## Why endpoint Hall is insufficient: an exact algebraic countermodel

Take B={r1,r2}, one paid hole r3, and seeds {s3,s1,s2}. Let
`k(r3)=s3`, `rho(s3)=r3`, `rho(s1)=u1`, `rho(s2)=u2` with distinct free
u1,u2. Allow only `r1->s3`, `r2->s3`, and `r3->s1 or s2`.
Each root separately reaches BOTH actual free endpoints. Endpoint-neighbor
Hall passes even for the pair, yet both paths need the SAME s3/r3 relay.
For X={r1,r2}, N_J(X)={s3}, so MC certifies the collision. This refutes
endpoint-factorization as a general algebraic shortcut.
