# Joint source tickets for every extreme-target demand

PROVED raw source injection on the closed MAXIMAL full-core domain. This
is not yet an injection compatible with all other demands/frozen owners.
The distinction is load-bearing.

## Domain and inherited B theorem

Let r>=7, M the genuine KEEP labels, mu=min M, nu=max M, and use an
original closed full B period with marked ancestor core size2r-1. All
original A operations/private nodes/anonymous ancestors remain arbitrary.
Use the accepted C15 density/all-SS/path-to-star source theorem at THIS
domain: every double has a marked grandparent and unmarked parent; marked
ancestors of the target become its children; all off-path marked parent
edges remain unchanged. No smaller-core extrapolation is made.

Consequently only the accessed marked target can GROW its set of marked
descendants at a genuine B operation. An old marked ancestor loses its
path-child branch and cannot gain another marked branch. Other marked
subtrees stay fixed. Nongenuine full KEEPs are B identities.

## Source recency lemma

Immediately before a genuine access x, let g be a strict marked B ancestor
of x. Since the previous genuine x access, g must have been genuinely
accessed: that previous operation put x at the B root, and only an access
to g could subsequently restore x as a descendant of g. After g's MOST
RECENT genuine access, there can have been no genuine access y strictly
between g and x in key order. Such a y is now in g's actual BST subtree
by interval convexity, but an access to y would have removed y from g's
descendants until another g access, contradicting recency.

For x=mu, every intervening genuine target is therefore GREATER than g.
For x=nu, every such target is SMALLER than g. Actual nongenuine KEEPs
repeat the most recent genuine root and have the same preservation property.

## Ticket rule

Each actual B double on an extreme-target access corresponds to its
distinct old marked ancestor g, by the inherited all-SS theorem. After
the last genuine access to g, A has root g, hence g is an A ancestor of x.
After the current original A access to x, it no longer is. Choose the
FIRST actual A rotation in between that removes g's ancestry of x.
Assign its literal StepEv/rotation bit `(t,j,b)` to that B demand `(s,k)`.

The selected rotation exists by AR1. By the recency lemma and AR2, it
occurs either in a DELETE or in the current extreme KEEP's A execution.
It cannot be supplied by an unrelated genuine KEEP or a nongenuine root
repeat. All h=0 identities remain in the source and create no ticket.

## Simultaneous noncollision

For a FIXED extreme x, the witness intervals lie after the preceding
genuine x and through the current x. These intervals are disjoint for
different x accesses. Within one interval, one rotation can remove at
most one marked g from x's ancestors. Thus all tickets for x are distinct.

Across the two extremes, a lost mu ancestor requires a RIGHT rotation,
whereas a lost nu ancestor requires a LEFT rotation. A single rotation
cannot have both directions. Two rotations in one StepEv use different
bit labels. Therefore ALL min/max demands, arbitrarily many repeated or
shared descriptions, have one simultaneous injection into actual A bits.

It follows, at this source scope, that

    Q_extreme <= 2 A_DELETE + 2 A_genuine-extreme-KEEP.

This is proved by a physical source injection, not used as its premise.

## Period, reflection and ownership limits

For an interval crossing the displayed cut, define the rule periodically
on the original closed trajectory and transport donor and demand IDs
to their unique occurrences in the ONE selected period. The rule is
period-equivariant. If two resulting IDs coincided, lifting one by the
period would contradict the just-proved injection on the actual repeated
trajectory. No physical unit from another copy is counted. This is the
same original cyclic/actual-ID convention as the accepted leaf channels.
Reflection interchanges mu/nu and left/right; arbitrary private A nodes
do not change AR1/AR2.

The resulting raw map is not asserted to extend natural h0, be unowned
under a0, or avoid units needed by interior B demands. A full source
comparison/frozen-owner extension still requires compatibility with the
interior demands and an actual licensed bank construction. The partial
map can be used in the original blocked-frontier exchange, which may
stop at an undefined interior owner; that is not a free terminal.

The theorem supplies a new source rule independent of private inventories
and prepared A states. It does NOT close C33 or the smaller-core cases.
