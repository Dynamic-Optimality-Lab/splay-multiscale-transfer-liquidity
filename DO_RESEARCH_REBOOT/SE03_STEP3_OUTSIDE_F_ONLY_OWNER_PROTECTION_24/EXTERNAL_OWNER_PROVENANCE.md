## Effective receipt origin lemma

Suppose the actual receipt is a0=l and a_i=a_{i-1} union{p_i->u_i},
where its successful licensed h_i/inverse-a_{i-1} walk ends at u_i.
Then for every noncommon paid owner p_i there is a unique payment stage,
and its receipt's LAST comparison edge is h_i(q_i)=u_i. In particular

    u_i in im(h_i).

Proof: by definition the terminal unit is the last comparison value.
Since each addition is at a free unit and all previous values are fixed,
that unit's first effective insertion is unique. No later addition can
take it. The owner is p_i, NOT generally the last comparison demand q_i.

Therefore R outside im(fE) has no common a owner, and every paid owner
there is an F-only initiating root with its actual first-use receipt.
It is incorrect to infer the owner is inside D merely because q_i or
the bit's birthplace is inside D. A nonlocal walk can enter that source
event from an outside root. Direct natural h0 locality does not prove
effective-owner locality after common exchange.

The only safely removable provenance classes are those actually excluded
by their receipt images. If an actual bit is outside im(fE) and outside
ALL historical comparison images used to append a, it is a-free. Proof:
common owners use im(fE); every other insertion uses an im(h_i) value.
This is a source-receipt exclusion theorem, not a promise those images
avoid the transported reserve. C22's ordinal3 producer exclusion is a
proved special image exclusion, not one for every reserve endpoint.

T19 preserves image, so it cannot improve this exclusion test by itself.
Creator and own-tail entries must be retained in the actual receipt
images; they are not assumed absent below a HIGH threshold.
