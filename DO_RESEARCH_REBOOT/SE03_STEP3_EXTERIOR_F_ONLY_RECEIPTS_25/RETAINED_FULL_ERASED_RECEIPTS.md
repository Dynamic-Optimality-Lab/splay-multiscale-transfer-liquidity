## Signs and root repeats outside the table

For an arbitrary retained KEEP, delta=qF-qE may have either sign. The
exact identities are |F_t\E_t|=(delta)_+ and |E_t\F_t|=(-delta)_+ under
the actual ordinal pairing. Owners require separate map/receipt fields.
A FULL-root repeat has qF=0, so cannot create F-only demands. An
AUXILIARY-root repeat can have qF>0: the actual t:G row above has qF1,
qE0. Root-repeat in A alone does not imply zero full cost either.

Every complete genuine full natural macro has H>=1: it starts after a
KEEP synchronizing A/B_F to the previous root and ends at a different
target. At least one A changing access is required. A single KEEP can
nevertheless have a_t=0 and positive qF, because its earlier original
DELETE pre-splayed the target. Its actual funding bits then belong to
that earlier positive tau, not to a nonexistent KEEP event. This is the
original H1 grammar, not an assumption that new B demand makes new credit.

Erased return does not force full B to return. Repeated blocks can
therefore carry retained discrepancies while erased B returns each time.
No uniform 'the two B executions rejoin quickly' bound is proved. The
complete owner disposition of those repetitions outside the safe receipt
class below remains OPEN.

## Exact useful bit at the v producer

Set u_v=(tau_v,1,1), the second actual A bit of v's unique positive
access. Full natural h0_F(kappa_v,2)=u_v. In a G block, the auxiliary
macro ending at v has exactly the six positive units of s,t,v, in that
order: t is only an auxiliary root repeat. It has qE<=1, so u_v is its
SIXTH unit and outside the auxiliary natural-own image. In an R block
v has its own two-unit auxiliary macro; its second bit is likewise not
aux-natural, but can be selected as a carry spare. Thus the stronger
canonical exclusion in CANONICAL_G_V_EXCLUSION is G-type only.

This distinction is essential; an R block does not inherit a G discard
property by having the same Q2/epsilon cost pair.
